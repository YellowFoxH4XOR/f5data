K33444422: Why thin provisioning is not recommended for BIG-IP VE on any hypervisor for production environments

Description

According to https://clouddocs.f5.com/cloud/public/v1/vmware/vmware_setup.html#virtual-machine-storage-requirements,

For production environments, virtual disks should be deployed Thick (allocated up front). Thin deployments are acceptable for lab environments.

This article tries to answer why we recommend full (thick) provisioning for production environments, and discusses the impacts and disadvantages of using thin provisioning.
 

Environment

  • BIG-IP VE on any hypervisor

 

Cause

The reason we say we need thick provisioning is because when using thin provisioning, the actual (physical) hard disk space is not allocated for the partition until it is used. This often creates problems when something is written to the (logical) HD on previously unused space if the host is not fast enough to allocate new HD space - similar to how "resizing a file" works on most operating systems, it can result in delays when attempting to write new data to disk inside the VM.

The fact is that any VMs should suffer from the same issue, whether they are BIG-IP or not. Adding to the confusion is the fact that since some of our competitors don't have any recommendations addressing this issue, there is a perception that their product is not affected by this. It's quite possible that some of our competitors don't consider this a problem, and in most VMWare environments, it probably won't even create a problem, but due to F5's high standards on performance issues, and the unpredictable nature of thin-provisioning (ie. unpredictable delays when writing to unwritten areas of the disk, because the host has to allocate the disk space for the "copy-on-write" file), we consider thin-provisioning to be unfit for production service (although it's considered fine for test labs, as long as they're testing functionality and not performance).
 

Recommended Actions

None
