Description

This article explains the sequence of events and actions taken by the F5 Distributed Cloud Security Operations Center (SOC) when a Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attack is detected and mitigated.
Customers may have different configurations for DDoS protection, such as "Always On" or "Always Available." The response and mitigation steps vary depending on the configuration.

Environment

  • F5 Distributed Cloud
  • DDoS protection
  • Customer configuration: "Always On" or "Always Available"
  • May include local router monitoring or route origination features

Cause

A DDoS attack is detected targeting the customer’s protected resources. The underlying source is malicious traffic intended to overwhelm or disrupt service availability.

Answer/Recommended Actions

For "Always On" customers:

  1. Distributed Cloud SOC receives an alert from monitoring devices.
  2. SOC inspects traffic to the targeted resource.
  3. If an attack is confirmed, mitigation begins (unless otherwise specified in Real-Time Incident Procedures [RTIP]).
  4. Customer is alerted per RTIP instructions.
  5. SOC provides ongoing updates about attack vectors and volumes, adjusting countermeasures as needed.
  6. Mitigation ends when the attack subsides.

For "Always Available" customers:

  • If using local router monitoring:

    1. SOC receives an alert from the customer’s router.
    2. Depending on RTIP, SOC may contact the customer before taking action or route traffic for further analysis by default.
    3. SOC inspects traffic for attack indicators.
    4. If an attack is confirmed, countermeasures are initiated.
    5. SOC provides updates about the attack.
    6. After the attack, mitigation ends and traffic is returned to the customer’s ISP.
      (See: How to Configure Local Router Monitoring with F5 Distributed Cloud Services)

  • For other configurations:

    1. Customer must advertise their route to F5 when an attack is detected.
    2. SOC is alerted based on attack severity, or customer may need to contact SOC.
    3. SOC inspects traffic traversing scrubbing centers.
    4. If an attack is confirmed, countermeasures are initiated.
    5. SOC provides updates about the attack.
    6. After the attack, mitigation ends and customer withdraws routes, returning delivery to their ISP.
