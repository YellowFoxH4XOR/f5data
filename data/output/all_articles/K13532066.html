Known Issue

Route domains may fail to function after you modify their parent/child relationships.

This issue occurs when all of the following conditions are met:

  • Your configuration contains multiple route domains.
  • You configure a route domain as a parent and another route domain as its child.

    For example, you configure RD20 as a parent route domain and RD10 as RD20’s child route domain.

  • You remove a child route domain's parent and reassign the parent as the child route domain's child.

    For example, following the naming convention from the previous example, you modify RD10 by setting the Parent Name to None. You then modify RD20 by setting the Parent Name to RD10.

  • You save the configuration or reboot your system.

The configuration may fail to load when you save the configuration or reboot the BIG-IP system.

Impact

The BIG-IP system fails to pass traffic for the affected route domains.

Symptoms

As a result of this issue, you may encounter the following symptom:

  • Your route domains fail to pass traffic after you modify them and load a new configuration or reboot your system.

Workaround

To work around this issue, you can delete the affected route domains and then recreate them with the new configuration.

Note: You must create a parent route domain before you can create a child domain associated with it.

Impact of workaround: The impact of the suggested workaround depends on the specific environment. F5 recommends that you test any such changes during a maintenance window and consider the possible impact on your specific environment.
