Topic

If the BIG-IP system has VLAN tagging enabled, it adds a 4-byte VLAN tag to packets. For the smallest packets, such as service pings, this results in a total packet size of 64 bytes.

Based on the IEEE 802.1Q specification, a switch that receives a tagged packet and then forwards the packet untagged should pad the packet to ensure that it meets the minimum frame size of 64 bytes. Certain Cisco switches do not perform this padding and will drop packets that are less than 64 bytes in size after the VLAN tag is removed.

Note: For more information, refer to the FN - 26096-WS-X6148-GE-TX, WS-X6548-GE-TX-May Drop Valid Packets on Egress-Hardware Modification Has Been Implemented Cisco article. This link takes you to a resource outside of AskF5, and it is possible that the information may be removed without our knowledge.

In addition to the Cisco switches specified in the Cisco knowledge base article, other Cisco and third-party switches may also experience a similar issue. If you suspect that this is the reason for dropped packets, contact F5 Technical Support, or contact your switch vendor for support.
