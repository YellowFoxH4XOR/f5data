Description

This article describes an issue where users may experience unexpected logouts or loss of session continuity when more than one pool member is enabled on a BIG-IP LTM virtual server. This can occur in environments where applications rely on a session identifier such as JSESSIONID, and traffic may be routed to different servers by upstream services like CDNs. With only a single pool member enabled, sessions remain stable; however, enabling multiple pool members may lead to session mismatches and immediate logout behavior.

Environment

  • BIG-IP LTM

Cause

When traffic from the same client is routed to different pool members, each server may maintain its own independent session state. If the application relies on a session identifier such as JSESSIONID, and persistence is not applied consistently, the session cannot be maintained across multiple servers. Standard cookie persistence may not provide adequate stickiness in these scenarios, especially when upstream components such as CDNs, proxies, or load distributors route requests to different pool members. As a result, sessions may be lost or immediately invalidated when multiple pool members are active.

Recommended Actions

To maintain session continuity when multiple pool members are enabled, configure persistence based on the session identifier used by the application. A universal persistence profile can be created to extract JSESSIONID (or another session token) from incoming HTTP cookies and apply consistent routing for all requests associated with that session. If requests may reach different pools or virtual servers, enable the appropriate match-across options to ensure that all traffic with the same session identifier is directed to the correct pool member.
