K10090015: Int Drops counter in AFM DoS Protection is incremented even though Mitigation Threshold is set to Infinite

Type
Support Solution
Published
2025-01-09
Updated
2026-05-13
Applies to
  • BIG-IP
  • BIG-IP AFM: 14.1.0, 14.1.X, 14.1.5, 14.1.4, 14.1.3, 14.1.2
Source
https://my.f5.com/manage/s/article/K10090015

Description

Under following conditions, you may see Int Drops (hardware drops) in AFM DoS Protection statistics to increment.

  • A DoS vector is configured as:
    • State: Mitigate
    • Threshold Mode: Fully Manual
    • Detection Threshold EPS/PPS: Inadequately lower than expected rate of traffic
    • Mitigation Threshold EPS/PPS: Infinite or very large
  • BIG-IP receives traffic that is over Detection Threshold and is well below Mitigation Threshold
  • Data Plane CPU usage is considerably high

Environment

  • AFM DoS Protection

Cause

When a rate of traffic is over Detection Threshold, BIG-IP considers those packets as an attack and such traffic is processed at a lower proiority compared to other non-attack traffic. While BIG-IP tries its best to process the lower priority packets, there could be a case where such packets being dropped while BIG-IP appears to have more hardware resource available due to the priority packet handling. When there is a drop due to this behavior, BIG-IP increments Int Drops in AFM DoS Protection statistics.

Recommended Actions

Configure Detection Threshold EPS/PPS higher than expected rate of traffic.

Additional Information

AFM DoS Protection uses a QoS feature for each DoS vector. When traffic received by a vector is higher than the Detection rate, the attack is reported. And when the traffic is reported, it is considered as attack traffic and it goes into low priority QoS ring. (In other words, attack traffic (detected by respective attack vector) being processed by the CPU will be given a lower priority compared to clean traffic.)  This becomes more challenging when CPU usage is already high (even under non-attacking period). For instance, if your CPU usage is 80% before attack got detected, then there is a high chance for the low priority ring to get full at an instant time. Once the ring buffer gets full, it starts dropping traffic and it’s reported in  “tmm.hsbe2_internal_pde_ring” tmctl table, under column name “ring_drop_stat”.
 
This behaviour is known as "QoS demoting". And when QoS demoting takes place, it is not necessarily dropping traffic. Drop happens only if the queue is full and CPU is not handling the packet fast enough to make more space for incoming packets. The ring drop is a system behaviour and therefore it’s not logged anywhere in the system. This mechanism exists to protect BIG-IP system from performance degradation or protect against system failure.
