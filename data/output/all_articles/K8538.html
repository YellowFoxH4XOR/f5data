Topic

FirePass 4100 and 4300 hardware platforms can be ordered with optional FIPS hardware (Cavium Nitrox XL 1005 NFB) to achieve FIPS-140-2 Level 2 and Level 3 compliant SSL/TLS processing.

In January 2003, the European Union adopted the Restriction of Hazardous Substances (RoHS) Directive. This directive, enacted in the EU on July 1, 2006, forbids the sale of all electrical and electronic equipment containing lead, cadmium, mercury, hexavalent chromium, polybrominated biphenyls, and polybrominated diphenyl ether in the EU after July 1, 2006, subject to certain limited exemptions.
 
F5 Networks, Inc. changed its manufacturing practices to ensure compliance by this deadline. This compliance effort resulted in a FIPS hardware card redesign in order to meet the RoHS standard. F5 Networks hired a third party testing authority, Atlan Laboratories, to validate conformance of the new Cavium Networks Nitrox XL FIPS cards used in F5 Networks FIPS products. In accordance with FIPS certification standards, the redesigned cards have been submitted by Atlan Laboratories on behalf of F5 Networks to the CMVP (NIST/CSE) for recertification. The request for certification was successfully submitted to the CMVP in 2007. Attached is a letter from Atlan Laboratories, confirming submission for FIPS certification. Certification was achieved in November of 2007 for Cavium Nitrox XL NFB hardware running firmware version 4.6.1.

FIPS certification ID numbers for Cavium Nitrox XL NFB products are 870 for Level 2 certification and 871 for Level 3 certification. The status of all submissions is publicly available and can be tracked on the CSRC NIST website, which is available at the following location:

http://csrc.nist.gov/

The FIPS hardware currently shipping with FirePass 4100 and 4300 controllers uses firmware version 4.6.1.

The FIPS hardware  on older, non RoHS-compliant FirePass controller units may have an older version of firmware, such as version 4.6.0 or 4.3.4. These firmware revisions are not FIPS-certified, but possess no difference in security functionality from the version of firmware that was submitted for certification. You can check the firmware version on a FirePass controller with FIPS hardware using the Display FIPS card memory dump feature from the Device Management: Maintenance: Troubleshooting Tools page of the Administrative Console. Alternatively, the memory dump can be performed from the Maintenance Console, using the FIPS card administration menu option, followed by the Display FIPS card memory dump menu option. The ASCII output of the memory dump should contain a firmware version string.
