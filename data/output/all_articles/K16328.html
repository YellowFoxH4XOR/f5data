Policy Information

Summary

A leap second is a second that is added to Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) to synchronize atomic clocks with astronomical time. The most recent positive leap second was introduced on December 31, 2016 at 23:59:60 UTC.

Description

The International Earth Rotation and Reference System Service (IERS) occasionally orders a leap second to be added to UTC time to compensate for a gradual decrease in the Earth's rotation around its own axis. The most recent leap second was December 31, 2016 at 23:59:60 UTC; the date of the next leap second to be introduced has not yet been announced. Before each leap second occurrence, F5 tests software versions that are currently supported with active software development, to validate the product handling of the leap second. Software versions that have not been tested are omitted from the Applies to box, located in the upper-right corner of the article.

This article provides information about how F5 products handle the leap second and whether there are any known issues related to the leap second.

Leap seconds are independent of leap days. For more information on leap days, refer to K59076876: The BIG-IP systems handing of leap days and leap years

F5 products handling of the leap second

The way F5 products handle the leap second depends on whether or not the device is configured to use the Network Time Protocol (NTP).

Systems configured to use NTP

F5 systems that are configured to synchronize their clocks with an NTP server will automatically process the leap second and adjust their clocks as expected.

For BIG-IP and Enterprise Manager systems that are configured to use an NTP server, the leap second will be processed automatically and logged to the system log. Two consecutive seconds will have the time stamp of midnight UTC immediately following the leap second. In this case, it would mean the timestamp 2017-01-01.00:00:00 UTC would last for two seconds.

Important: BIG-IP systems running 11.2.1 through 11.2.1 HF14, 11.3.0, 11.4.0, and 11.4.1 through 11.4.1 HF8, BIG-IQ systems running 4.0.0 through 4.3.0, or Enterprise Manager systems running 3.0.0 through 3.1.1 HF4, may experience a reboot during the leap second event if the system is configured to use NTP. For more information about this issue as well as the workaround and fixes, refer to K16839: The BIG-IP, BIG-IQ, or Enterprise Manager system may reboot when configured to synchronize its clock with an NTP server.

Systems not configured to use NTP

For F5 systems that are not configured to synchronize their clocks with an NTP server, the system will not correct the time for the leap second, and the time reported by these systems will have a one-second difference relative to UTC after the leap second correction.

Known issues

The following articles provide information about F5 products and versions that may experience issues related to the leap second adjustment:

  • K11816: The BIG-IP system may prematurely remove a persistence record if the system time is changed
  • K16839: The BIG-IP, BIG-IQ, or Enterprise Manager system may reboot when configured to synchronize its clock with an NTP server

Recommendations

F5 recommends that you configure your device to synchronize its clock with an NTP server if your device is not running an affected BIG-IP version listed in K16839: The BIG-IP, BIG-IQ, or Enterprise Manager system may reboot when configured to synchronize its clock with an NTP server. If your F5 system is not configured to synchronize its clock with an NTP server, you should manually reset the clock after the leap second occurs.
