Known Issue

Diameter messages larger than 1024 bytes may cause the Traffic Management Microkernel (TMM) to produce a core file.

This issue occurs when all of the following conditions are met:

  • You send a diameter message with attribute-value-pairs (AVP) larger than 1024 bytes.
  • You change or manipulate the AVP; for example, you use an iRule.

Impact

The BIG-IP system may temporarily fail to process traffic as it recovers from TMM restarting, and systems configured as part of a high availability (HA) group may fail over.

Symptoms

As a result of this issue, you may encounter one or more of the following symptoms:

  • The TMM process restarts and produces a core file in the /var/core directory.
  • The BIG-IP /var/log/tmm file indicates that TMM is restarting with a SIGSEGV.
  • The BIG-IP system generates TMM core files to the /var/core directory, with a time stamp that correlates to the traffic interruption.

Workaround

To work around this issue, ensure messages are less than 1024 bytes.

Impact of workaround: Performing the suggested workaround should not have a negative impact on your system.
