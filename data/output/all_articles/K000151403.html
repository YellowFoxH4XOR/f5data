K000151403: After HA failover, one Virtual Server’s traffic continues to land on the standby unit

Type
Support Solution
Published
2026-08-31
Updated
2026-08-31
Applies to
  • BIG-IP
  • BIG-IP LTM
Source
https://my.f5.com/manage/s/article/K000151403

Description

After a high availability (HA) failover on F5 BIG-IP LTM, certain Virtual Servers (VS) may show as "up" on the newly active unit but do not receive client traffic. Instead, network traffic for these VS continues to be routed to the standby unit. Packet captures can reveal that affected VS traffic is not reaching the active node, and ARP behavior may appear abnormal for impacted VIPs. Other virtual servers in the same partition and VLAN function normally and send gratuitous ARP (GARP) as expected.

Environment

  • HA pair with active/standby failover
  • Virtual servers configured within the same partition and VLAN
  • Trunk (aggregate) interfaces used for upstream connectivity
  • Link Aggregation Control Protocol (LACP) enabled for trunk interfaces

Cause

This issue is caused by a misconfiguration or physical problem either on the upstream switch or with one of the trunk member interfaces on the BIG-IP device. For example, if one interface in the trunk (such as 1.1) is down while another (such as 1.3) remains up, GARP packets sent after failover may originate from the MAC address of the down interface. If this occurs, upstream switches may not properly update their ARP/MAC tables, resulting in continued routing of traffic to the standby or an incorrect path.

Recommended Actions

  • Check all interfaces participating in each trunk/aggregate group on both BIG-IP and upstream switches.
  • Ensure every trunk member interface is physically up and correctly configured at both ends.
  • Review LACP status to confirm all members are synchronized and operational.
  • Address any physical issues by replacing cables/SFPs or reseating modules where necessary.
  • After correcting configuration or hardware issues, perform a failover test to verify GARP packets are sourced from an active interface and that upstream devices update their ARP/MAC tables accordingly.
  • For troubleshooting purposes, consider moving problematic VIPs into a separate traffic group.
  • Coordinate with your network team to validate that switch configurations match those of BIG-IP trunks and that all connected switch ports are healthy.
