Description

How do BGP communities work for customer routing preferences? 

Environment

  • F5® Distributed Cloud (XC) DDoS
  • Silverline
  • BGP
  • Traffic Engineering

Cause

F5 Distributed Cloud (XC) allows customers to influence routing preferences and traffic engineering by attaching specific BGP community values to their advertised prefixes.

Recommended Actions

Customers may choose to send approved BGP communities with their advertised prefixes in an effort to control how and where traffic is returned to them. These communities will adjust local preference on our routers and determine the "best path" in the customer route table for returning traffic down a GRE tunnel (or backbone to GRE tunnel).

Silverline

  • Higher local preference is "more preferred", lower local preference is "less preferred".
  • F5's default local preference for customer advertisements is 180.
  • Backbone traffic (site-to-site within 55002) is local preference 160.
  • Local preference is a non-transitive attribute (it is local only to that router).
  • BGP communities in this article are referring to affecting 55002 local preference used to route traffic to customer backend, and does not affect 55002 local preference set to our carriers for attracting client traffic.

Example of Route-maps with BGP Communities:

route-map customer-primary permit 10
match ip address prefix pl-route-to-f5
set community 55002:90 additive

route-map customer-backup permit 10
match ip address prefix pl-route-to-f5
set community 55002:70 additive

Then, apply the 'customer-backup' route-map to the F5 BGP neighbor statements on the routers at your backup site and apply the ‘customer-primary’ route-map on the routers at your primary site.

 

To see communities in the ASN:MM format you need to set in the global config:

ip bgp-community new-format

It is important to mention that F5 recommends to have <= 100 local preference on advertisements from your Internet Service Provider (ISP) to the internet if you want to always prefer F5 over your ISP.

 

For Specific Upstream Peering:

XC provides a rich set of DNA (Do Not Announce) and de‑preference (Depref) communities that allow customers to control how traffic ingresses into F5 XC by attaching specific communities to their route announcements. The complete and most up‑to‑date list of supported communities is available in our WHOIS record. This list is regularly updated as XC adds new PoPs, providers, and peering exchanges.:

Example#1 De-Pref in ME:


To depref a prefix in Middle East by prepdng 2 times  add this community  to your announcment 35280:1072  


 


Example#2 DePref in Canada:


To depref a prefix in Canada by prepdng 3 times  add this community  to your announcment 35280:2143


 


Example#3 DNA to Telstra:


To completely block  a prefix to Telstra  add this community  to your announcment 35280:10069


 


Note:  Please note that different providers may have diff interpretation of such communities. e,g if a customer adds to DNA (35280:2149)  in Canada, while the prefix will not be announced by F5 pops in Canada, other providers who recive that prefix from F5 in USA, may still announce there, although it may be with a longer AS-path. Similar things applies with other DNA/Dpref communities. 






Additional Information


 


Prefix Announcement and Traffic Engineering On  F5 XC: 


Here are the available options for customers to perform traffic engineering using tunnels


 


  •  Prefer All Paths from XC to Customer Equally: No additional configuration required.
  • 

    Advertise More‑Specific Prefixes : 
    

    Customers may choose to announce more specific prefixes (upto /32) via preferred session/interface. Please note that customers still need to announce min  /24 prefixes so it can be announced to internet.
    



 


  • 

    One Primary, Others Backup (LP‑Based):
    

    On primary do not add any community, but on all backup, add the following communities.
    

    route-map map-route-to-f5xc permit 10
 match ip address prefix-list route-to-f5xc
 set community 35280:50
 exit 

router bgp 64512
  neighbor 100.99.x.x route-map map-route-to-f5xc out
    



The default value of LP for customers prefixes is 10,000. With 35280:50, it sets LP to 9950.
