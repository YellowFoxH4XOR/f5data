Security Advisory Description

While there are many cyber-threats creating a constant need for cybersecurity efforts, history teaches us that geopolitical conflicts often generate increased cyber activity. In recent years the world has seen conflicts in Ukraine, Yemen, Iran, and elsewhere generate corresponding cyber activities. Whether it is deliberate actions by one party in the conflict looking to disrupt their foe and their allies, or independent hacktivism intended to support one side, or protest the conflict in general, it all creates increased demand for cybersecurity.

As a company dedicated to cybersecurity, F5 closely monitors reports of any cyber activity associated with global conflicts and stands ready to assist anyone who comes under cyberattack. F5 provides this assistance through F5 SIRT and F5 Distributed Cloud Services. If you are under attack, do not hesitate to call and engage F5 for assistance. For information on how to submit a support case, refer to K2633: Submit a support case and K000135931: Contact F5 Support.

During times of conflict, F5 has witnessed an expansion of distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks against infrastructure and media outlets, attempts to deliver malware to end-user devices (these are commonly delivered through phishing or spear-phishing attacks), web defacement in support of one side of the conflict, and several misinformation and disinformation campaigns delivered through electronic means such as email and SMS messages.

Operational readiness

F5 suggests that you ensure that your existing processes and documentation (playbooks, emergency contact lists, site surveys, and so on) are up to date and that everyone in your organization understands their role and responsibilities should an attack occur.

Work to ensure that you have visibility of how your organization's infrastructure behaves during a period of normal use. The best time to start logging and understanding performance, throughput, and traffic patterns is before a cyber-security attack to ensure that you know what baseline you are working from and how it can be best set to quickly spot abnormal patterns. F5 products such as BIG-IP LTM, BIG-IP AFM, and BIG-IP Advanced Web Application Firewall (WAF) can help you understand this baseline. If you are already subscribed to an on-demand DDoS scrubbing service (such as F5 Distributed Cloud Services), you should ensure that you have tested and validated the process to migrate traffic to and from this service.

Security posture

Global conflicts may emerge at any time, and cyber campaigns may begin before any public awareness, so it is important to be proactive, rather than reactive, in securing our networks.

Set antivirus and antimalware programs to conduct regular scans

  • BIG-IP APM can enforce endpoint posture checks for antivirus software.
  • BIG-IP Advanced WAF and BIG-IP LTM integrate with Internet Content Adaptation Protocol (ICAP) servers to run virus scans on binary content being uploaded or passed through the BIG-IP system.

Enable strong spam filters to prevent phishing emails from reaching end users

  • F5 does not offer a spam filtering product; however, you can use the Secure Web Gateway feature of BIG-IP APM, protocol inspection features of BIG-IP AFM, the DNS filtering features of BIG-IP DNS, and the functionality of BIG-IP SSL Orchestrator to apply policies to any outbound traffic generated by your endpoints (for example, if someone attempts to visit a malicious link contained within a phishing email).

Update software

  • BIG-IQ can assist in keeping your BIG-IP install base up to date.
  • BIG-IP products support live updates for IP Intelligence, Advanced WAF signatures, threat campaigns, and more.

Filter network traffic

F5 products can help you filter network traffic both inbound and outbound from your network:

  • BIG-IP LTM and BIG-IP AFM both offer network filtering policies and DDoS protection.
  • BIG-IP Advanced WAF and F5 Distributed Cloud WAF can filter HTTP traffic to block malicious requests and include L7 DDoS protection.
  • Distributed Cloud Services can scrub inbound traffic in the cloud before it ever reaches your organization.
  • You can develop custom iRules for BIG-IP and NJS scripts for NGINX to block traffic based on various characteristics.

In addition, F5 recommends the following to improve security posture:

  • Ensure that all F5 products within your estate are kept up to date and running the latest release within your chosen branch, and, of course, ensure that you are running the most recent, supported branch that you are comfortable using.
  • Ensure that you regularly patch other infrastructure, both hardware and software, within your organization and that you subscribe to the security announcement lists for all your vendors. For information about subscribing to F5 Security mailing lists, refer to K9970: Subscribe to email notifications regarding F5 products and security announcements.

Follow the principles of least access for F5 hardware and software systems:

  • Management interfaces are only exposed to trusted, secure networks, and ideally access is limited only to designated administrative devices to prevent pivot attacks.
  • Access is restricted to trusted users only, with only the permissions necessary.
  • Only required services are exposed to the network.
  • Where possible, host subsystem access is disabled.

DDoS attacks

DDoS attacks can cause organizations downtime, denying access to some or all services to some or all users and customers. They can cause organizations financial damage by consuming resources, potentially triggering expensive auto-scaling activities in public or private clouds.

F5 can help mitigate DDoS attacks in several ways:

  • BIG-IP AFM or Distributed Cloud Services can mitigate volumetric network attacks.
  • Advanced WAF, Distributed Cloud Services, and NGINX can be used to mitigate DDoS attacks against applications and services delivered over HTTP(S).
  • Many F5 products, such as BIG-IP AFM, Advanced WAF, NGINX Plus, and Distributed Cloud Services support geolocation blocking, which can, where appropriate, reduce the ability of an attacker to cause harm.
  • BIG-IP AFM and Advanced WAF both support F5 IP Intelligence, allowing you to drop traffic quickly and easily from known malicious hosts, such as known botnets, spam sources, and so on.
  • A guide to mitigating DDoS attacks using NGINX is also available.

If you already have F5 BIG-IP products in your estate, you may want to review the Attack mitigation matrix using F5 security products and the HTTP Brute Force Mitigation Playbook – Chapter 1.

Defacement attacks and web application attacks

Cyber adversaries may want to compromise web applications to deface them and replace the legitimate content with their own message, but they can also provide a route into a target organization for further compromise. If an adversary can compromise a web application so that they can run their malicious code on the webserver, they have gained a foothold within the organization from which they can then attempt to pivot to deploy second-stage payloads such as ransomware or wipers.

Advanced WAF and Distributed Cloud Services can effectively mitigate such attacks through signature and normalization routines and rapid updates to signatures and threat campaigns when our research teams discover new threats.

Advanced WAF and Distributed Cloud Services offer advanced anti-bot protection mechanisms that you can use to stop automated traffic from reaching your web services. Both also offer brute force attack protections built into bot protection in Distributed Cloud Services and as a separate feature in Advanced WAF.

To get the most benefit from your existing Advanced WAF deployment, F5 recommends the following:

  • Keep your attack signatures up to date using automatic updates where possible.
  • Ensure that you have moved all appropriate signatures into blocking mode and that the overall policy is set to block.
  • Carry out regular monitoring and policy fine-tuning.
  • Log to a remote system.

The BIG-IP ASM operations guide contains more information on achieving these steps.
