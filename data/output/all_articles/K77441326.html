Description

This Article explains the details of BGP timers such ase BGP keep-alive, hold-down, connect, advertisement-interval, BGP Table scan interval, Route-refresh timer and others.

Cause

Recommended Actions

BGP is an exterior routing protocol which is used in an ISP environment to exchange or advertise routes among ISPs and the enterprise.

BGP Keep-Alive Timer

This is the heartbeat timer where the local neighbor sends a BGP heartbeat packet in a recurring interval to a remote neighbor to check the availability of the neighbor. By default this interval is 30 seconds.

For example:

2020/06/26 22:40:24 informational: BGP : 10.125.129.34-Outgoing [ENCODE] Keepalive: 9 KAlive msg(s) sent
2020/06/26 22:40:25 informational: BGP : 10.125.129.34-Outgoing [DECODE] KAlive: Received!
2020/06/26 22:40:54 informational: BGP : 10.125.129.34-Outgoing [ENCODE] Keepalive: 10 KAlive msg(s) sent
2020/06/26 22:40:55 informational: BGP : 10.125.129.34-Outgoing [DECODE] KAlive: Received!
2020/06/26 22:41:24 informational: BGP : 10.125.129.34-Outgoing [ENCODE] Keepalive: 11 KAlive msg(s) sent
2020/06/26 22:41:25 informational: BGP : 10.125.129.34-Outgoing [DECODE] KAlive: Received!

BGP Hold-Down Timer

This is the timer for the local neighbor to wait before declaring the remote neighbor as unavailable. By default, this interval is 90 seconds which is three times the Keep-Alive Interval. In other words, if the local neighbor misses 3 consecutive Keep-Alive packets from the remote neighbor then the local neighbor considers the remote neighbor as unavailable and temporarily changes the BGP status to Idle, followed by removing all associated routes advertised by the neighbor from the Routing table/BGP table.

For example:

2020/06/26 22:42:25 informational: BGP : 10.125.129.34-Outgoing [DECODE] KAlive: Received!
2020/06/26 22:42:54 informational: BGP : 10.125.129.34-Outgoing [ENCODE] Keepalive: 14 KAlive msg(s) sent
2020/06/26 22:43:24 informational: BGP : 10.125.129.34-Outgoing [ENCODE] Keepalive: 15 KAlive msg(s) sent
2020/06/26 22:43:54 informational: BGP : 10.125.129.34-Outgoing [ENCODE] Keepalive: 16 KAlive msg(s) sent
2020/06/26 22:44:12 errors: BGP : [SOCK CB] sock_getpeer() failed (107:Transport endpoint is not connected), FD(10)

The BGP neighbor status change stages before and after hold-down timer expiry. By default, the hold down timer is set to 90 seconds, and after the default timer the local neighbor goes through different stages such as Idle, Connect, and Active.
Initially the status changes to Idle which indicates that BGP detects a start event and tries to initiate a TCP connection to the BGP peer. After 5 seconds BGP changes the status to Connect which indicates that BGP has initiated the TCP connection, and after 10 seconds the status changes to Active.

Wireshark Analysis during Idle, Connect, and Active stages

During the Connect stage, a TCP SYN will be sent to the peer and will keep retransmitting during the Connect stage time which is 10 seconds. After 10 seconds, the status will be changed to Active and local neighbor will send TCP RST to the peer. After 75 seconds of Active status, a TCP SYN will be sent again to the peer and status will be changed to Connect.

BGP Advertisement Interval

The BGP Advertisement interval is a timer that decides how much time must elapse between an advertisement/withdrawal of routes to a BGP peer. The default is 30 seconds for eBGP peers and 5 seconds for iBGP peers. This can be changed with the on a per neighbor basis.

Additional Information

BGP is different from an Interior routing protocol in the way of establishing adjacency, implement routing policies and route exchange. BGP has a vast implementation of attributes to implement complex routing policies. BGP is also widely used with the MPLS implementation in the provider core or as a PE-CE routing protocol. BGP supports both IPv4 and IPv6
