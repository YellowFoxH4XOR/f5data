Topic

When you troubleshoot physical connectivity issues with RJ45 cables, it can sometimes be difficult to determine whether the cable or the plug is the source of the issue. This is often due to the wide range of variance in manufacturing processes across different vendors; in many instances, neither component is faulty but the cable manufacture may not be terminating their cables within the specified tolerances of the following standards:

  • ANSI/TIA-1096
  • ANSI/TIA-968
  • International standard: ISO-8877

The following F5 hardware uses copper ports that strictly adhere to these standards:

  • B4200 (Puma 2)
  • B4200N (Puma 2 NEBS)
  • 1600 (Mercury)
  • 3600 (Mercury)
  • 3900 (Mercury)
  • 5000 series (Whitethorne 1U)
  • 7000 series (Whitethorne 2U)

Description

Although the standards specify certain tolerances for the jacks and connectors, in some instances a cable connector built at the lower end of the tolerances may fail to make a solid connection when inserted into a jack that is manufactured at the upper end of the tolerances. A failed connection can also occur when too much pressure is applied while crimping the connector to the cable; the gold contacts may recess further than the specification and fail to make contact effectively with the jack.

Recommendations

When you experience intermittent physical connectivity issues, F5 recommends that you check for any looseness of a cable connector that is fully inserted into a jack, as this may indicate a poor fit. You may also try using another cable, preferably from another vendor or batch when possible.
