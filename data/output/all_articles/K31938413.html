Description

This document provides a more specific information about packet drops counters available in tmctl utility mentioned in K10191: Troubleshooting packet drops. For a complete list of options available for troubleshooting the packet drops please refer to the parent article

Environment

  • BIG-IP seen or suspected of dropping packets

Cause
Multiple possible causes exist, further analysis required

Recommended Actions

Note: Not all tables are available on all BIG-IP platforms

  1. Check tmm_stat, e. g.:
    tmctl tmm_stat --select=dropped_packets

    dropped_packets
    0
    • dropped_packets refers to packets dropped by TMM code

  2. Check interface_stat, e. g.:
    tmctl interface_stat --select=name,counters.drops_in,rx_hw_drop,tx_hw_drop

    namecounters.
    drops_in    		rx_hw_droptx_hw_drop
    1.1000
    1.22722622722620
    2.1000
    …
    • counters.drops_in refers to ingress packets dropped
    • rx_hw_drop refers to ingress packets dropped by hardware
    • tx_hw_drop refers to egress packets dropped by hardware
  3. Check ifc_stats, e. g.:
    tmctl ifc_stats --select=name,in_discards,in_errors,in_unknown_protos
    namein_discardsin_errorsin_unknown_protos
    1.1000
    1.2000
    2.1000
    …
    • in_discards refers to ingress packers discarded at their arrival
    • in_errors refers to ingress packets containing errors in their format
    • in_unknown_protos refers to packets with protocol field containing an unknown value

  4. Check ip_stat, e. g.:
    tmctl ip_stat --select=dropped,nbr_pbq_full_dropped,nbr_unreachable_dropped

    droppednbr_pbq_full_droppednbr_unreachable_dropped
    000

    • nbr_unreachable_dropped refers to packets that cannot be sent out because the Data Link Layer address of the next hop cannot be resolved (e. g. no answer to the ARP requests)

    • nbr_pbq_full_dropped refers to packets that cannot be sent because of too many packets already in the queue waiting until the Data Link Layer address of the next hop becomes available

    • Check tmm/ether_stat, e. g.:
      tmctl -d blade tmm/ether_stat --select=name,rx_drop_interface_no_vlan

      namerx_drop_interface_no_vlan
      1.117
      1.22
      2.10
      …
      • rx_drop_interface_no_vlan refers to packet drop situations in that the interface belongs to no VLAN, or a VLAN tag does not exist in your packet but the interface belongs to a VLAN. You may refer to the Additional Information section for detailed scenarios.
    • Check dos_stat, e. g.:
