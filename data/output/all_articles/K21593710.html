Description

APM SSL VPN can be establish either over TCP (a.k.a. TLS tunnels) or over UDP (a.k.a. DTLS tunnels).
If the VPN is configured to use DTLS, the client machine will try to establish the VPN over UDP at the beginning of the negotiation.
In case of any difficulty, the client machine falls back to TCP to establish the VPN.

When BIG-IP APM is a virtual machine hosted into any AWS environment, the client machine may fail to establish the VPN over UDP, even though the BIG-IP configuration is OK.

Environment

  • BIG-IP APM or BIG-IP Access Manager
  • BIG-IP is a Virtual Machine running in the AWS cloud
  • BIG-IP (NetworkAccess, VirutalServer, ...) has been configured to permit VPN to get established over UDP

Cause

The issue may be due to the type of AWS instance running BIG-IP APM:
The type "c5x.large" may prevent the VPN to get established over UDP.

Recommended Actions

You can attempt switching from "c5x.large" to an instance type "m4.2xlarge".
In order to get more insight over those types of machines or should you still face any difficulty at having the VPN established over UDP, F5 Networks recommends engaging assistance from AWS to troubleshoot such a situation.

Additional Information

Here is the synopsis of the DTLS negotiation, that shows why the client and the BIG-IP cannot establish the VPN over UDP (analysis of the DTL Straffic only).  A packet capture will show the following:

  1. Client sends the "Client Hello" message with Sequence Number: 0
  2. BIG-IP replies with "Hello Verify Request" message with Sequence Number: 0 and with Cookie: 1849cf...
  3. Client receives the "Hello Verify Request" message with Sequence Number: 11068160 and with Cookie: 1849cf...

Notice that the "Sequence Number" is somehow changed on its way to the client.
Because the "Sequence Number" the client is getting is not the expected sequence number, the client resumes the DTLS handshake at point #1.  After 3 unsuccessful attempts, it will switch to TLS.
