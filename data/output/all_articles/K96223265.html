Description

After configuring an IKEv2 IPsec tunnel with multiple subnets, only one of the configured networks may work.

The specific scenario is uncommon and is caused when multiple selectors are offered in one child SA. In this article it will be referred to as "multi-selector".

When multiple selectors are offered in an ISAKMP exchange, the BIG-IP will pick the first one. The other subnets offered by the remote peer are not considered to be part of the subsequently established tunnel and traffic to those other subnets will not be permitted by the BIG-IP.

Multiple subnets between two IPsec peers is a common requirement and is supported by the BIG-IP, but not via the multi-selector method.
 

Environment

  • IKEv2 IPsec with multiple subnets (multi-selector) configured.
  • Most network vendors do not support multi-selector, however:
    • A Strongswan remote peer is known to support this feature.
    • Google Cloud and AWS may also support multi-selector,
    • At the time of writing F5 Support has only observed multi-selector employed on Strongswan.

Cause

A Strongswan IPsec peer can be configured as so in ipsec.conf:
 

conn mytunnel
<snip>
   rightsubnet=10.100.1.0/24,10.100.3.0/2
   leftsubnet=10.100.2.0/24,10.100.4.0/24

Such a configuration will case a proposal to have multiple selectors in the TSi (Traffic Selector - Initiator) and TSr (Traffic Selector - Responder payloads. The result of the configuration is intended to mean that not only 10.100.1.1 can talk to 10.100.2.1, but also that 10.100.1.1 can talk to 10.100.4.1.

The standard way is to define traffic-selectors separately so that they have unique SPIs. The networks associated with one SPI cannot talk to subnets associated with another SPI. This then causes multiple tunnels to be established; one per configured selector.
 

Recommended Actions

Strongswan and other vendors support the standard way of defining traffic selectors such that one selector starts one tunnel. For each pair of networks that need to communicate, define a separate traffic-selector.

For example, the Strongswan would be configured with "also" statements that reference the IPsec connection and append a selector:

conn mytunnel
<snip>
   rightsubnet=10.100.1.0/24
   leftsubnet=10.100.2.0/24
 
conn mytunnel-also
   also=mytunnel
   rightsubnet=10.100.3.0/24
   leftsubnet=10.100.4.0/24
