Topic

At F5, we've established a comprehensive global methodology for problem investigation and resolution. This 6 step approach is versatile, enabling the troubleshooting of a wide range of issues, from complex network problems to emerging or unfamiliar technologies. It is also flexible allowing for the order of the steps to be interconnected and updated as the troubleshooting progresses.
 
These 6 steps are interconnected creating a dynamic loop that helps guide problem solving to find root causes. Insights from later stages, like data collection, can reshape initial understandings or refine problem details, creating essential feedback loops. This can lead to updates in the system's mapping and hypotheses, prompting revised testing strategies. Through this iterative process, each cycle refines the focus and enhances understanding.
 
F5 Support invites you to join us in applying this methodology when Contacting F5 Support, ensuring that the solutions we develop target the root cause of issues, not just the symptoms. Your active participation can make all the difference in turning complex challenges into clear solutions. In fact, it also helps seed our knowledge base with answers to emerging topics or issues that we see rising in the F5 Services organization.  You can read more about that in F5 and Knowledge Centered Service (KCS®)
 

Description

 

 
The troubleshooting process involves several key steps: stating the problem, specifying it in detail, mapping the system's flow, identifying possible causes, collecting data, and analyzing it to confirm or refute possible causes. Outcomes of this process can include ruling out a cause, confirming a cause without reaching the root cause, or identifying the root cause itself. Identifying root cause is a journey that may require revisiting previous steps based on new information. We appreciate when customers stay engaged through this iterative process, as your ongoing feedback helps refine the path to resolution.
 
These steps provide a common framework when trying to explain systems, identify problems, and find resolutions.
 
Step 1: State the Problem
Step 2: Specify the Problem
Step 3: Map the System
Step 4: Develop possible causes
Step 5: Test and prove/disprove suspected cause(s)
Step 6: Iterate through until the root cause is identified
 
The six steps can be summarized into three overall goals:
  • Steps 1 through 3 establish a clear understanding of the problem and the system involved
  • Steps 4 and 5 involve formulating and testing theories about the issue
  • Step 6 confirms the root cause and the extent of the solution needed

Recommendations

Step 1: State the Problem
Define the problem clearly and quantitatively, accounting for expected versus actual outcomes. For example, "The application is running slowly," could be more clear by asking questions of the problem, and prepares us for step 2.  What is the exact load time or throughput rate and how does this compare to the expected performance? We encourage customers to articulate the problem in their own words, as this helps both you and our support team establish a common understanding from the start. 
 
Step 2: Specify the Problem
Clarify the issue by addressing key questions that highlight deviations from expected outcomes. While not all questions may be relevant, exploring different angles can provide new insights and help identify the root cause of the problem. We find that when customers provide detailed descriptions it enhances collaboration and helps our team tailor the support to your unique situation.
 
Some questions we might ask ourselves:
      • What: Pinpoint the exact component or process affected and describe the issue clearly.
      • Where: Identify the issue's logical and physical location within the network and its environmental context.
      • When: Determine the exact start time, frequency, and pattern of the issue.
      • Extent: Assess the scope, severity, and trend of the issue across affected components.
 
Step 3: Map the System
Understand and document the technology's operational steps to identify potential failure points and data collection needs. A simple outline or detailed flowchart can guide troubleshooting efforts and identify gaps in understanding, therefore making complex problems more manageable. Break down each step for detailed understanding, which aids in identifying root cause or gaps in our understanding. 
 
You will find many examples of F5 relying on system maps to describe complex technologies throughout many of our offerings: 
      • K000136055: Configuring and Understanding TCP profile timeout values
      • VELOS High Availability
      • F5 NGINXaaS for Azure: Multi-Region Architecture
      • K8082: Overview of TCP connection setup for BIG-IP LTM virtual server types

We suggest customers sketch a simple outline of the affected system or process. Sharing this with our support team can significantly speed up the troubleshooting process and ensure we're all on the same page

 
Step 4: Develop possible causes
Using the system map from Step 3 allows us to scrutinize each element to identify potential causes, asking how each could contribute to the observed issue.  When considering potential causes, it's crucial to logically link each one to the observed effects, eliminating any that are contradicted by existing knowledge. This helps in refining the list to more probable causes. You know you are on the right track when you can answer questions like:
 
      • Is the explanation plausible and simple, or is it complex
      • What assumptions underlie the cause, and does it align well with the observed effects
 
Prioritize causes that are clear, minimally assumptive, and align with the problem's symptoms for testing.  We welcome customers to share any theories or observations they might have. This collaborative approach often leads to a more comprehensive understanding and quicker resolution.
 

Step 5: Test and prove/disprove suspected cause(s)

Select a potential cause from Step 4 and determine appropriate tests or data collection methods to support or refute the theory. Consider the expected outcomes if the cause is accurate, while also being mindful of the test's impact and any potential side effects that could hinder further investigation. Some methods for a great Step 5 include comparing the suspected cause against existing knowledge, seeking evidence, and conducting experiments.

Testing should aim to affirm or dismiss the cause with minimal assumptions and clear alignment with the problem's symptoms.  We encourage customers to engage with this step as much as they're comfortable. Firsthand insights can be invaluable and contribute significantly to the diagnostic process.

Step 6: Iterate through until the root cause is identified

Identifying the root cause in complex systems requires iterative questioning and analysis, typically using the "5 Whys" technique, drilling down through layers of why questions until a cause is found that, if resolved, prevents recurrence of the problem. This approach helps distinguish between mere symptoms and the underlying issue.

The true root cause should account for all symptoms, be manageable, and prevent future occurrences.
