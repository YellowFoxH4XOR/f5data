Description

In a BIG-IP LTM High Availability (HA) environment configured with BGP routing and dual border leaf switches, you may experience unexpected traffic interruptions when one of the border leaf switches is restarted or taken offline. During such events, the BIG-IP system does not immediately reroute the traffic to the alternate available path, resulting in a temporary service disruption. Traffic flow remains interrupted until BGP reconverges and the original connection is restored.

Environment

  • BIG-IP LTM

  • High Availability (HA)

  • Border Gateway Protocol (BGP)

  • Dual border leaf switches

Cause

Traffic disruption occurs due to the default BGP hold timer configuration:
 
When a directly connected border leaf switch is rebooted or fails, the BIG-IP does not immediately detect the BGP neighbor loss.
This delay is caused by the default BGP hold timer value, which defines how long the system waits before declaring the neighbor as "down."
Routes associated with the failed neighbor are not promptly removed, delaying BGP failover to an alternate path and causing dropped traffic.

Answer/Recommended Actions

To address these issues and improve failover behavior, you should review and modify several BGP parameters as needed:
 
1. Optimize Hold Timer and Keepalive Interval
Adjust the hold timer and keepalive interval for faster BGP failure detection:

  • Set the hold timer to a lower value (e.g., 30 seconds) to reduce the delay before detecting a failed neighbor and removing its routes.
  • Adjust the keepalive interval to 10 seconds to ensure timely communication between BGP peers. 

2. Verify TTL Settings
Ensure that the Time-to-Live (TTL) value is configured correctly for your topology:

  • For directly connected eBGP peers, use the default TTL of 1.
  • For peers that are not directly connected, adjust the eBGP multihop setting to allow a higher TTL value.

3. Review Neighbor and Route Configuration
Double-check all BGP neighbor and route definitions to ensure proper configurations:

  • Verify that the ASN (Autonomous System Number) is set correctly for each peer.
  • Confirm the IP addresses of neighbors match your topology.
  • Ensure routes are being properly advertised and installed.

Additional Information

If service interruptions persist after these changes, further troubleshooting may be required in conjunction with F5 Support. 
For additional insights into BGP configuration and route tuning on BIG-IP, refer to the BIG-IP Networking and Routing Management documentation.
