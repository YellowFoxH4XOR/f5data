Known Issue

The Link Layer Discovery Protocol (LLDP) daemon fails to re-attach to the MCP daemon (mcpd) when mcpd is restarted. As a result, the lldpd process may not function as expected.

This issue occurs when all of the following conditions are met:

  • LLDP is enabled on the BIG-IP system.
  • The mcpd process is restarted.

LLDP can be enabled in the Configuration utility by navigating to Network > Interfaces > LLDP > General. In addition, you may verify whether LLDP is enabled on the BIG-IP system by typing the following command:

tmsh list /sys service lldpd

When LLDP is enabled, the following output may be observed:

Service         Boot          Default
lldpd            on            yes

Impact

No LLDP information will be exchanged with the adjacent devices.

Symptoms

As a result of this issue, you may encounter the following symptoms:

  • The BIG-IP system may log warning messages on every LLDPDU received into the /var/log/ltm file. The messages may appear similar to the following example:

    lldpd[12353]: 01570005:4: Received LLDPDU from an unknown interface :1.8
    lldpd[12353]: 01570005:4: Received LLDPDU from an unknown interface :1.8
  • The LLDP neighbor information will not be updated.

Resolution

Workaround

To work around this issue, you can restart the lldpd service. To do so, perform the following procedure:

Impact of workaround: None.

  1. Log in to the BIG-IP command line.
  2. Restart the lldpd service by typing the following command:

    tmsh restart /sys service lldpd

After completing this procedure, you may notice that the warning messages are no longer logged to the /var/log/ltm file.
