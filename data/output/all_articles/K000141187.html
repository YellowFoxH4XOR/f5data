Description

You can notice F5 BIG-IP is the inability to see high throughput peaks in the performance graphs, even though logs, such as those found in /var/log/ltm, indicate that the licensed throughput has been exceeded. For example, an alert might be logged showing that 75% of the licensed throughput was exceeded multiple times, but these spikes do not appear on the throughput graphs in the GUI.

Bandwidth utilization is <number of Mbps> Mbps, exceeded <%> of Licensed <number of Mbps> Mbps

Environment

  • BIG-IP LTM

Cause

F5 BIG-IP collects throughput statistics over defined sampling intervals, with the smallest interval available in the GUI being one minute. During this interval, all data is averaged, which smooths out any brief peaks that might have occurred.

Additionally, bandwidth on the F5 represents the total sum of both incoming and outgoing traffic at the same time. Some graphs in the GUI may display incoming and outgoing traffic separately, so it’s important to account for both when analyzing the overall bandwidth usage. If there is a sudden spike in throughput that lasts for a short duration (e.g., a few seconds), it may significantly affect the momentary traffic but not enough to change the one-minute average shown on the graph. This aggregation and averaging are necessary to prevent resource exhaustion on the system, as collecting throughput data at a finer granularity (milliseconds or seconds) would require substantial CPU, memory, and storage resources, potentially overloading the system.

Recommended Actions

Given this behavior, we recommend the following actions to monitor bandwidth effectively:

  1. Rely on Log Alerts: Use log alerts from /var/log/ltm to monitor when the throughput or bandwidth thresholds are exceeded. These logs provide a real-time snapshot of the momentary spikes that the graphs may not capture due to averaging.
  2. Configure SNMP Monitoring: Consider setting up SNMP monitoring for bandwidth or throughput to collect more granular and frequent samples. This method allows for real-time monitoring and provides more detailed data, helping you catch and analyze short-lived peaks in throughput.
