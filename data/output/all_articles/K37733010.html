Description

When you configure a BIG-IP with a mirroring IP address that does not enable the mirroring functionality. It simply provides a route by which mirroring can take place. To enable the mirroring it has to be enabled on the specific configuration objects where the traffic needs to be mirrored. It is done this way to avoid unnecessary overhead in the network, to reduce resource usage on the BIG-IP, and to not duplicate the action of protocols such as HTTP and TCP that will automatically reconnect a broken connection making it look and appear seamless to the user.

There are also times that disabling mirroring is necessary. But just removing the mirroring IP(s) and not disabling it at the configuration objects can have unintended consequences.

Environment

  • Failover, connections, persistence, or traffic
  • Virtual Servers, SNAT, Vlan, Persistence Profiles, SSL Session IDs
  • Mirroring

Cause

  1. Traffic that needs to be mirrored, such as long lived connections or other non HTTP or TCP traffic that is essential, and needs to be mirrored. Or risk that traffic being dropped in then event of a failover.
  2. There are times that you may need to disable mirroring. This may be because of a platform migration where the BIG-IPs in the Device Service Cluster (DSC) are now on dissimilar platforms. When this is the case persistence will not work. Or you may be having issues on VIprion Blades and the guests, in the same DSC, are resourced differently. In the short-term this is likely not a problem because the DSC peers will all be brought up within hours of each other in most cases. But if there were some reason this was prolonged persistence mirroring would need to be disabled.
