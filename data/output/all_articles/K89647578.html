Known Issue

The iRules commands may fail to drop a connection or may trigger a SIGSEGV that causes the Traffic Management Microkernel (TMM) process to restart and produce a core file.

This issue occurs when the following condition is met:

  • You use the following iRules commands within a client-side event.
    • drop
    • discard
    • reject

When you use these iRules command within a client-side event, either an unexpected SYN packet is forwarded to the server, or the dropped connection flow results in a SIGSEGV sent to TMM.

Impact

  • If an unexpected SYN packet is forwarded to the server, the BIG-IP system subsequently resets the server's SYN-ACK response with a reset cause of TCP 3WHS rejected.
  • A SIGSEGV signal sent to TMM causes the TMM process to restart. The BIG-IP system temporarily fails to process traffic as it recovers from the TMM restart, and devices configured as a high availability (HA) pair may fail over.

Symptoms

As a result of this issue, you may encounter one or more of the following symptoms:

  • F5 iHealth lists Heuristic H616310 on the Diagnostics > Identified > Medium screen. 
  • Your BIG-IP system fails to drop a connection and forward an unexpected SYN to the server. Your BIG-IP system will subsequently reset the server's response with a reset cause of TCP 3WHS rejected.
  • A SIGSEGV signal is sent to TMM and causes the TMM process to restart.
  • The BIG-IP system logs an error message in the /var/log/tmm file that appears similar to the following example:

    notice ** SIGSEGV **

  • Your BIG-IP system temporarily fails to process traffic as it recovers from the TMM restart.
  • If configured for HA, the affected BIG-IP device fails over to a configured peer device.
  • Your BIG-IP system generates a TMM core file to the /shared/core directory, which includes a time stamp that correlates with the traffic interruption.
