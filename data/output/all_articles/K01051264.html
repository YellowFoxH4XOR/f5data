Known Issue

All VIPRION blades may become disabled when netHSM timeouts occur.

This issue occurs when all of the following conditions are met:

  • You have a VIPRION system with one or more blades installed.
  • You have configured the BIG-IP system to protect Secure Sockets Layer (SSL) keys using remote netHSM.
  • Your VIPRION system is experiencing high memory usage.
  • Your VIPRION system is unable to connect to the remote netHSM.

Impact

Intermittent SSL handshake failures may occur initially, and traffic processing may completely fail due to all installed blades entering a disabled state.

Symptoms

As a result of this issue, you may encounter one or more of the following symptoms:

  • Remote clients experience intermittent SSL handshake failures.
  • All traffic processing has failed and all VIPRION system blades are in a disabled state.
  • Your VIPRION system has failed over to a high availability (HA) peer.
  • In the /var/log/ltm file, you observe a logging event similar to the following example:

    tmm[12345]: 01010025:2: Device error: crypto codec fips-crypto0-0 queue is stuck.
    sod[6789]: 01140029:4: Resuming log processing at this invocation; held 10 messages.
    sod[6789]: 01140029:4: HA crypto_failsafe_t fips-crypto0-0 fails action is failover.
    sod[6789]: 010c0052:5: Standby for traffic group /Common/traffic-group-1.
    sod[6789]: 010c0018:5: Standby

Workaround

There is no workaround. To restore traffic processing, you can reboot all blades installed in the VIPRION system. To do so, perform the following procedure:

Impact of workaround: Performing the following procedure will cause all traffic processing to stop while the system is rebooted.

  1. Log in to the Advanced Shell (bash) of the affected BIG-IP VIPRION system.
  2. To reboot all of the installed blades, type the following command:

    clsh reboot
