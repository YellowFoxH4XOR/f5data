Description

  • How to manipulate the advertisement of BGP routes to F5 Distributed Cloud scrubbing centers.
  • Note: at all times the customer has full control of their routing configuration, and this document should serve only as a guide.  There are many alternate ways to achieve the same results as detailed here. IP addresses are subject to change.  Please contact the SOC if in any doubt.

 

Environment

  • F5® Distributed Cloud (XC) DDOS

Resolution/Answer

It's very important that the BGP advertisements are configured so that in the event of an attack, the route to F5 Distributed Cloud scrubbing centers are preferred. 



For customers under  AA service : Under normal operations both in and out traffic goes through customer ISP(s), but during an attack after XC start announcing customer prefixes, in bound traffic flows through internet >>XC >> customer.

For customers under AO Service: Use F5 as your primary for inbound.

 

This can be achieved in 2 approaches :

 

1. Use Summary to ISP and Specific Routes to XC: 

Many  XC customers have larger prefix say 100.64.0.0/18 (Summary routes) which they break up into multiple specific routes say, into /23s and /24s. They often want DDOS protection for their some or all specific prefixes. In that case we recommend that 

  • The announce only the summary routes to their ISPs
  • If they have multiple ISPs they can use AS-Prepends to de-pref one ISP over another. 
  • Then they should use announce specific and/or summary prefixes to XC depending on what they want mitigated. 
  • This ensures that traffic to these prefixes on internet are preferred via XC. 

 

2. Use  AS‑Path Prepend on session to local ISP/This is preferred method

If customers don't have the option to as above, e.g if a customer has only /24s, they may use as-prepend to announce 

  1. Create the configuration to add pre-pends to the local carrier advertisement (see example below). If customer has multiple ISPs, they can use  different AS-Prepend sizes to de-pref one ISP over another.
  2. Test the pre-pends by routing traffic through F5 and validate for potential route leaks. 
    1. If route leaks are present, it is essential to validate with the local upstream carrier whether they honor AS-PATH prepends. It is important to note that many carriers may not honor AS-PATH prepends and instead use BGP communities, which often take precedence over AS-PATH prepends when announcing routes to the internet. In such scenarios, it is recommended to consult directly with the Internet Service Provider (ISP) to ensure proper handling of route advertisements.
  3. Choose different paths on XC as described in What are the F5 BGP Communities for Customer Routing Preferences and Traffic Engineering?

Note: The routed solution is asymmetric routing.  Inbound traffic to your prefixes will be routed through F5 by virtue of the preferences route advertisements we send upstream. We do not advertise any internet routes to customers, and your ISP(s) remain the default route for outbound traffic.  

 

Additional Information

Example: Cisco AS Path Prepending

As noted, there are many different ways to do this (and on different platforms than Cisco) but the concepts are the same. Here's an example of how you would setup AS Path Prepending with Cisco to set your BGP preferences to prefer the F5 Distributed Cloud scrubbing center.

ip prefix-list customer-public-routes permit CUSTOMER_PREFIX_1

ip prefix-list customer-public-routes permit CUSTOMER_PREFIX_2

 

route-map map-route-to-f5 permit 10

match ip address prefix-list customer-public-routes

set as-path prepend 65001 65001 65001 65001    # assume 65001 is your AS 

 

Then, to route traffic over to F5, you’d apply the route-map to the BGP neighbor you have with your ISP. 

 

To Activate:

### apply activation configuration ###

router bgp 65001  #your AS number

  neighbor xxx.xxx.xxx.xxx route-map CARRIERONE-f5-on out .  #this is the IP of your ISP BGP peer

  neighbor f5-dca prefix-list route-to-f5 out

  neighbor f5-sjc prefix-list route-to-f5 out


### clear bgp neighbors ###

clear ip bgp peer-group f5-dca soft out

clear ip bgp peer-group f5-sjc soft out

clear ip bgp xxx.xxx.xxx.xxx soft out

 

Note: Repeat this for all upstream peers you have and this will ensure the BGP advertisements for your networks have the AS-path prepended, whereas the one to F5 won’t, ultimately meaning that the F5 route will be preferred.

 

To Deactivate:

 ### apply deactivation configuration ###

router bgp 65001           #whatever your ASN is

  no neighbor xxx.xxx.xxx.xxx route-map CARRIERONE-f5-on out

  neighbor f5-dca prefix-list deny-all out

  neighbor f5-sjc prefix-list deny-all out


### clear bgp neighbors ###

clear ip bgp xxx.xxx.xxx.xxx soft out

clear ip bgp peer-group f5-dca soft out

clear ip bgp peer-group f5-sjc soft out

 

Additional Information

Alternatives to AS Prepends:

  • Use a BGP community on the announcements to one ISP that asks that ISP to selectively prepend towards some of their customers or peers or in some regions. The large carriers typically have such communities.
  • Another option is to use the 'no-export' tag on the route-map that you do not want propagated in the Internet.
