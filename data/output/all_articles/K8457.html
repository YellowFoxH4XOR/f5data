Topic

This article discusses system behavior when you enforce connection limits that you have configured on virtual servers, pool members, or nodes for a Clustered Multiprocessing (CMP) system.

Description

The BIG-IP system divides the configured connection limit by the number of Traffic Management Microkernels (TMMs) that are running on the system and forces the calculated limit to round down to the nearest whole number, per TMM. If the connection limit is not evenly divisible by the number of running TMMs, the remainder (<connection limit> % <number of TMMs>) is distributed, one per TMM, to the lower ordered TMMs. For example, when an object is configured with a connection limit of 10 on a CMP system with four running TMMs, the BIG-IP system divides the configured limit of 10 by four TMMs and calculates a connection limit of two per TMM. The remaining (10 % 4) two connections are then distributed to the lower ordered TMMs resulting in a calculated connection limit of three for tmm0 and tmm1. This is expected behavior.

Similarly, on chassis systems, when connection limits are not evenly divisible by the number of running TMMs, the remainder is distributed among lower ordered TMMs. However, because blades may leave and rejoin the cluster, the overall calculated connection limit may be lower than the configured connection limit by as much as the remainder.

In some cases, your BIG-IP system may cause uneven distribution of pool connections per TMM due to Disaggregation (DAG) or OneConnect profile configuration. The DAG (CMP hash algorithm) requires randomness in the source or destination port. Each TMM instance enforces its share of the connection limit independently; therefore, traffic must be evenly distributed across all TMM instances through the DAG configuration to achieve 100% utilization of the connection limit. If traffic is not evenly distributed in the DAG, some TMM instances may reach their configured connection limit sooner than expected.

Note: With very low connection limits, or if the number of connections is low compared to the number of TMM instances, the BIG-IP system may appear to enforce lower than expected connection limits. In addition, F5 recommends that you do not configure a connection limit to a number lower than the number of TMM instances supported by the platform; doing so may result in unpredictable connection counts.
