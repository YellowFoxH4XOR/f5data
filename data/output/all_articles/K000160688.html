Description

On BIG-IP LTM devices (i5k series), an increase in the counters.rx_ffp_pdisc packet discard counter may be observed. This counter reflects packets discarded by the hardware before reaching the TMM (Traffic Management Microkernel) and is generally considered cosmetic if there is no service impact.

Environment

  • Product: BIG-IP LTM
  • Version: 17.1
  • Platform: i5k series
  • Monitoring of counters.rx_ffp_pdisc via system outputs

Cause

The increase in the rx_ffp_pdisc counter is expected and is due to the hardware discarding packets that are irrelevant, malformed, or not intended for processing by the BIG-IP. This counter is specific to platforms with Broadcom switch hardware (such as the i5k series) and does not appear on i2k series devices due to architectural differences.

Answer/Recommended Actions

  • No action is required if there is no service impact.
  • The increase in the rx_ffp_pdisc counter is cosmetic and does not indicate a problem with the BIG-IP device.
  • It is recommended to:
    • Review network configurations to ensure unnecessary or irrelevant traffic is not being forwarded to the BIG-IP interfaces (e.g., avoid bridging unnecessary traffic onto BIG-IP VLANs).
    • If further investigation is desired, perform a packet capture on the switch port connected to the BIG-IP to identify the source of discarded packets, as these will not appear in captures taken directly on the BIG-IP.
    • Optionally, check and replace network cables if hardware issues (such as invalid checksums) are suspected.

Additional Information

  • The rx_ffp_pdisc counter stands for "Receive Fast Forward Path Packet Discard." It increments when the hardware (Fastpath/PVA or HSB chip) discards a packet before it reaches the TMM software.
  • Common reasons for incrementing this counter include:
    • Packets with invalid checksums, illegal headers, or MTU violations (Layer 2/3 integrity issues)
    • DAG (Disaggregated Directed Acyclic Graph) hardware failures in determining the correct TMM core
    • Packets arriving on interfaces or VLANs that are down or disabled
    • Hardware filtering based on global drop rules (e.g., invalid IP fragments)
  • The absence of this counter on i2k series devices is due to the lack of Broadcom switch hardware.
