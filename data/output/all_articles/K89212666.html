Known Issue

A dynamic Bandwidth Control (BWC) policy may cause the Traffic Management Microkernel (TMM) to restart and produce a core file.

This issue occurs when all of the following conditions are met:

  • You have configured a virtual server with a dynamic BWC policy.

    Note: BWC policies are static by default; the Dynamic setting is disabled.

  • TMM attempts to enforce dynamic change in rate for multiple traffic instances.

Impact

The BIG-IP system may temporarily fail to process traffic as it recovers from the TMM restart. Devices configured as a high availability (HA) pair may fail over.

Note: Occurrences of this issue are extremely rare.

Symptoms

As a result of this issue, you may encounter one or more of the following symptoms:

  • Your BIG-IP system logs messages to the /var/log/ltm file that appear similar to the following example:

    notice BWC: instance already exist

    notice ** SIGFPE **

  • Your BIG-IP system fails over to the standby system if configured as a HA pair.
  • Your BIG-IP system generates a TMM core file to the /shared/core directory.

Workaround

To work around this issue, you can use a static BWC policy.

Impact of workaround: A static BWC policy enforces the maximum rate for combined traffic and does not guarantee fairness bandwidth for each session. The impact of the suggested workaround depends on the specific environment. F5 recommends testing any such changes during a maintenance window with consideration to the possible impact on your specific environment.

Note: Bandwidth control policies are static by default; the Dynamic setting is disabled. After a policy is enabled as dynamic, you cannot disable the Dynamic setting. Therefore, you must create a new static policy and associate the new policy with virtual servers that use the affected dynamic policy.
