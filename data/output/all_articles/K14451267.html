Description

A BIG-IP can terminate, inspect and re-encrypt a TLS connection. Is it possible to decrypt the traffic between two IPsec peers when the BIG-IP is an intermediary (passthrough/forwarding) device?

 

Environment

  • Two IPsec peers, neither one being the BIG-IP itself.
  • An IPsec tunnel exists between the peers.
  • A BIG-IP forwards the ISAKMP and ESP or AH packets between the peers.
  • The IPsec tunnel uses encryption.

 

Cause

It is not possible to decrypt encrypted packets on the BIG-IP because the encryption keys are not known or seen by the BIG-IP, even during negotiation.

 

Recommended Actions

The peers must expose the encryption key in some way to the administrator. Most vendors have the ability to drop encryption and authentication keys into logs, when the right debug level is enabled.

If both peers are under the control of the BIG-IP administrator, the BIG-IP could be configured as a peer for both sides of the connection. Meaning, the BIG-IP has a unique tunnel to Peer A and a unique tunnel to Peer B. At that point, the BIG-IP can see the packets as they pass from one tunnel into the next tunnel.

If the IPsec tunnel has null encryption ciphers then the ESP or AH packets are not encrypted and can be viewed as plain text in the packet.

 

Additional Information

Much like TLS, IPsec is designed against monster-in-the-middle attacks.
