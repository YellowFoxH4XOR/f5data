Known Issue

The Traffic Management Microkernel (TMM) sends a gratuitous ARP (GARP) request for the floating IP address when the system transitions from active to offline.

This issue occurs when the following condition is met:

  • Your BIG-IP system that is configured as a high-availability (HA) traffic group member transitions from active to offline.

Note: For BIG-IP 11.5.3, this issue was introduced in 11.5.3 HF2. For BIG-IP 11.6.0, this issue was introduced in 11.6.0 HF6.

During an HA failover event, the GARP request that the device going offline sent may arrive on neighboring devices after the GARP request that the newly active device sent. As a result, after the HA failover event, the neighboring device's Address Resolution Protocol (ARP) cache for the floating IP address may resolve to the MAC address of the offline BIG-IP system.

Impact

The BIG-IP system may experience traffic interruptions following an HA failover event.

Symptoms

As a result of this issue, you may encounter one or more of the following symptoms:

  • After an HA failover event, a neighboring device's ARP cache for the floating IP address may resolve to the MAC address of the offline BIG-IP system.
  • Your BIG-IP system may experience traffic interruptions after an HA failover event.

Workaround

To work around this issue, you can configure the BIG-IP system to use a MAC masquerade address for the traffic group. To do so, refer to K13502: Configuring MAC masquerade (11.x - 14.x).

Impact of workaround: Configuring a MAC masquerade address for a traffic group can temporarily disrupt traffic on the associated VLAN, while the surrounding network devices learn the new hardware address association. F5 strongly recommends that you perform this procedure during a scheduled maintenance window.
