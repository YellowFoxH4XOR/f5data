Topic

This article discusses amendment order licensing behaviour. For more information on mapping VE registration keys after a subscription renewal, refer to K12183049: Mapping VE registration keys after a subscription renewal which describes standard renewal mapping scenarios. Amendment scenarios follow a different licensing behaviour.

Description

It is important to note the difference between standard renewal mapping behaviour vs amendment order scenarios.

In amendment scenarios, the requirement for activation is driven by amendment design (net-new entitlement behaviour), and is not influenced by sequencing, recovery actions, or processing variations.

When an amendment order introduces a product or quantity change:

  • The system creates new entitlements
  • The system generates new activatable keys. The keys
    • are standard activatable licenses
    • are not eligible for mapping or swap operations
    • will appear in a Ready to Activate state
  • Existing keys are not carried forward via mapping.
    There is no supported method to convert these activatable keys into mapped or swap keys.

Clarification on key types

Not all newly generated or visible keys are swap keys. In amendment scenarios involving product or quantity changes, keys should be activatable keys unless explicitly stated otherwise.

The activation of these keys is required and should not be interpreted as a mapping or swap operation.

Customer impact (Critical)

The activation of newly generated keys requires license reactivation on BIG-IP systems. Depending on your deployment and activation method, this may introduce service impact or downtime.

It should be stressed that activation is a mandatory step for amendment-generated keys, with any service impact dependent on the your deployment architecture.

Sequencing Requirements

The correct sequence for processing renewal and amendment activities must be followed:
Renewal --> Mapping --> Amendment
If amendment orders are processed before renewal mapping is completed:

  • This may create an irrecoverable state
  • The order may require RFC and rebooking to recover. This can result in delays and additional operational overhead

Important: While incorrect sequencing does not change the final licensing behaviour (new activatable keys will still be generated), it directly impacts process recoverability and operational complexity.

Business impact

Misinterpreting licensing behaviour (mapping vs activation) can result in the following:

  • Risk of unplanned service impact or downtime due to required license activation
  • Increased operational overhead, including rebooking due to sequencing errors.
