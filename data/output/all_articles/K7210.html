Topic

H.323 based call software (commonly used in Voice over IP and IP based video conferencing) typically uses TCP port 1720 to initiate a call. TCP port 1720 is the call signaling channel. Once the call is established, additional TCP and UDP connections are established to handle data, such as audio and video.

The H.323 based signal channel on TCP port 1720 remains connected from the beginning to end of the entire call session, but typically remains idle during this period.

Note: This behavior is similar to FTP, in that a control channel is used to send commands and a separate data channel is established to send and receive data. The control channel is subject to long idle periods in which it is not used while data is being transferred.

Since the signal channel can remain idle for long periods of time, the TCP timeout period in the TCP profile can close down the connection. As a result, the call is terminated due to its primary channel closing. The default TCP profile timeout is 300 seconds (5 minutes).

Recommendations

To allow for the TCP port 1720 signal channel connection to remain idle for longer periods without being terminated and terminating the call, you can increase the Idle Timeout value in the TCP profile used by the virtual server.

Note: F5 recommends that you create a custom TCP profile instead of modifying the default TCP profile. For information, refer to K14488: Working with profiles.

Impact of recommended action: The impact of the suggested workaround will depend on the specific environment. F5 recommends testing any such changes during a maintenance window with consideration to the possible impact on your specific environment.
