Description

The BIG-IP is frequently generating alarms for the IPSec VPN tunnels configured on it, going UP and DOWN.

However, no service interruption is observed for the tunnels, and the team supporting the device on the tunnel peer, doesn't observe any flaps.

When checking the tunnels status with tmsh show net ipsec shows the tunnel state as UP.

Logs observed are similar to:

/var/log/ltm

01010297:5: tmm IPsec: Tunnel down <remote-address> - <local-address>
01010297:5: tmm IPsec: Tunnel down <remote-address> - <local-address>

/var/log/ipsec.log

017c0003:5: tmm IPsec: Tunnel down <remote-address> - <local-address>
017c0008:5: IPsec Tunnel DOWN destination(<ip-address>) source(<ip-address>) reqid(<id>)
017c0003:5: tmm IPsec: Tunnel down <remote-address> - <local-address>
017c0008:5: IPsec Tunnel DOWN destination(<ip-address>) source(<ip-address>) reqid(<id>)

Environment

  • IPsec terminated on BIG-IP
  • IPsec tunnels are UP, but logs suggest tunnel is flapping

Cause

An IPsec tunnel here is actually two Security Associations (SAs), one each for inbound and outbound data over the tunnel. A log message saying the "tunnel" is up or down is actually a log message saying that SAs have been added or deleted. The log message is slightly misleading because the tunnel remains up as long as mature SAs exist to handle data flow.

When SAs are near the end of their lifetime, the so-called "soft lifetime", the IPsec peers will negotiate replacement SAs. Once the new SAs are established (causing a tunnel 'up' log), the old SAs will eventually be deleted when the "hard lifetime" is hit. The deletion of those old SAs will cause a log saying that the tunnel went 'down'. This is by design and does not necessarily mean the tunnel went down.

On a BIG-IP, SAs will be deleted when one of the following happens:

  • The full lifetime of the SAs are reached.
  • The peer sends an early delete for an SA.
  • The BIG-IP administrator deletes SAs manually.
  • The BIG-IP administrator reconfigures the IPsec tunnel config.

Recommended Actions

None. This is expected behaviour. However, the logs can be filtered by following K35284961: Filtering BIG-IP log messages, or you can disable the SNMP traps indicating the Tunnels are down, following K11234: Disabling default SNMP traps.

If in doubt, confirm the tunnel is up by checking the related traffic-selector.
