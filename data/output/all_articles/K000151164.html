K000151164: Observing one VELOS Chassis power supply unit (PSU) power input/output significantly higher than others

Description

On VELOS Chassis platform, a significant power supply unit (PSU) load imbalance may occur, where one PSU (for example, PSU 3) draws substantially more power (e.g., approximately 1800W) than the others (all less than 300W). System logs may display events such as "PSU input under-voltage fault PMBus event", "PSU input under-voltage warning PMBus event" and related PMBus communication faults. The issue persists even after reseating the PSU, and swapping PSU positions causes the high load to follow the specific PSU.

Environment

  • VELOS Chassis PSU power input/output fault

Cause

The root cause is a faulty PSU that does not participate in proper load sharing with other PSUs in the chassis. This results in one PSU drawing excessive current and power while others remain at low loads. The problem is confirmed when moving the suspect PSU to another power bay causes the high load to follow it. Input under-voltage events or data center power fluctuations may trigger or reveal this behavior, but persistent imbalance indicates a hardware failure within the affected PSU.

Recommended Actions

  1. Isolate and Test Suspect PSU:
  • Remove the suspect PSU from the chassis.
  • Observe if remaining PSUs balance their loads correctly.
  • Reinsert the suspect PSU after waiting at least 30 seconds before reapplying power.
  • If imbalance recurs on reinsertion, proceed to next step.
  1. Swap Positions for Confirmation:
  • Verify that there are an adequate number of redundant PSUs in your chassis.
  • If there are adequate redundant PSUs, swap the suspect PSU with another power bay.
  • If there is no redundant PSU, failover all Tenants hosted on the chassis and shutdown the chassis before swapping PSU with another power bay.
  • If high load follows the same physical unit regardless of power bay location, this confirms a hardware fault in that specific PSU.
  • Initiate an RMA process for replacement of the affected/faulty PSU.
