Description

Running an Nmap scan on a Virtual Address may result Nmap reporting that all ports are open.

Syncookie or DOS activation logs may appear in the /var/log/ltm logfile.

err tmm7[13506]: 01010252:3: A Enforced Device DOS attack start was detected for vector TCP SYN flood, Attack ID 1167369740.
notice tmm6[13506]: 01010253:5: A Enforced Device DOS attack has stopped for vector TCP SYN flood, Attack ID 1167369740.
 
Environment
  • BIG-IP AFM
  • DOS protection
  • syncookie enabled (hardware or software).

Cause

This is expected behavior of syncookie protection.
Nmap can trigger the TCP syncookie protection feature. By default, with no traffic passing through the BIG-IP, Nmap is less likely to trigger syncookie protection. But the combination of normal traffic and Nmap can tip the protection thresholds over the limit and cause the BIG-IP to start issuing syncookies. Nmap interprets the resulting SYN/ACK packet as an indication of availability for that service. If the port scanner (such as Nmap) tried to complete the three-way handshake with a port that no Virtual Server is listening on, the client will find that the BIG-IP refuses to establish the connection.

Recommended Actions

The following steps will reduce the likelihood that a port scanner such as Nmap will trigger syncookie protection.

  • The recommended method is to back off the rate at which the scanner, such as Nmap, sends SYN packets.
  • There are several detection ways that syncookies can be activated. Check the /var/log/ltm log to determine whether DOS detection (if BIG-IP AFM is provisioned), or syncookie mode started during the scan. Adjust the threshold accordingly.

Additional Information

  • Throughput testing may trigger DOS protection, depending on the protocol being tested.
  • Intermediate firewalls or proxies can cause false-positive port scan results if they respond on behalf of the scan target.
