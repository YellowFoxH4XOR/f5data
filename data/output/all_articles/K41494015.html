Known Issue

A Virtual Clustered Multiprocessing (vCMP) guest may experience a lack of CPU resources due to vCMP host processes assigned with an incorrect priority.

This issue occurs when all of the following conditions are met:

  • You have provisioned the vCMP module on the vCMP host.
  • You have deployed vCMP guests with two or more cores.
  • The vCMP guests are running BIG-IP 11.5.0 or later.
  • The vCMP host is running BIG-IP 12.1.0
  • The HTSplit feature is enabled on the vCMP guests, which is the default setting.

Impact

The vCMP guest may experience a lack of CPU resources for essential BIG-IP processes. If the vCMP guest is a member of a high-availability device group, an unexpected active-active state or an unexpected failover may occur.

Symptoms

As a result of this issue, you may encounter one or more of the following symptoms:

  • BIG-IP iHealth lists Heuristic H610148-1 (vCMP guest) on the Diagnostics > Identified > High screen.
  • BIG-IP iHealth lists Heuristic H610148 (vCMP host) on the Diagnostics > Identified > Medium screen.
  • A vCMP guest fails over to another device in the device group.
  • The vCMP guests in a device group experience an unexpected active-active state.
  • You see repeated log messages in the /var/log/ltm file of the vCMP guest that appear similar to the following example:

    warning sod[1234]: 010c0083:4: No failover status messages received for 3.100 seconds, from device /Common/example-device.com (172.16.1.2) (unicast: -> 172.16.1.1).

  • The output of top -Hbn1 on a vCMP host indicates that the qemu-kvm process has the highest nice value (listed under the NI column), meaning the lowest priority.

    For example:

    PID USER      PR  NI  VIRT  RES  SHR S %CPU %MEM    TIME+   P COMMAND
    1234 qemu      39  19 6361m  60m 2748 R 15.9  0.1  46:39.64  1 qemu-kvm
    1235 qemu     -97  19 6361m  60m 2748 S 15.2  0.1  46:37.61  0 qemu-kvm
