Description

A rolling upgrade is designed to minimize downtime by performing the upgrade in a sequential and non-disruptive manner.

This is often used in high-availability (HA) configurations, where traffic can be shifted between instances or chassis modules without interrupting service.

Environment

  • VELOS (F5OS-C)

Additional Information

1. Rolling Upgrade

Key Features:

  • Occurs gradually, upgrading one service instance, chassis partition, or node at a time.
  • Traffic is steered away from the instance being upgraded, ensuring continuous application availability.
  • Ideal for production systems requiring high availability or minimal disruption.

Steps in a Rolling Upgrade:

  • The system first upgrades the standby instance (or an instance with no traffic).
  • After the standby instance is verified to be running correctly on the new version, traffic is redirected to the standby.
  • The process is repeated for the active instance, ensuring traffic is always handled by a functional unit.
  • This process continues across the whole cluster or chassis (if applicable).

Advantages:

  • Minimal or no downtime.
  • Maintains traffic flow and application availability during the upgrade as much possible as it can. Traffic is interrupted briefly during the failover from one system controller to the other.
  • Fault-tolerant: If something goes wrong in the upgraded unit, active traffic remains unaffected.

Drawbacks:

  • Slightly more complex process compared to an out-of-service upgrade.
  • Ensures only a single unit is upgraded at any given time, which might extend the total upgrade duration.

2. Out-of-Service Upgrade

  • An out-of-service upgrade takes the system completely offline during the upgrade. This approach is appropriate for systems where downtime is acceptable or when there are no redundant/HA systems in place.

Key Features:

  • The system is stopped, upgraded, and restarted after the upgrade process is complete.
  • Simple, fast, and direct compared to rolling upgrades.
  • May involve preparing for downtime or scheduling a maintenance window.

Steps in an Out-of-Service Upgrade:

  • Traffic is stopped or drained before initiating the upgrade.
  • The system is completely taken out of service (offline).
  • The upgrade is performed on the entire system.
  • Once the upgrade is complete, the system is restarted and traffic is restored.

Advantages:

  • Straightforward and faster upgrade process.
  • Less complexity, as the entire system is upgraded at once.
  • Suitable for non-production environments or systems with low traffic.

Drawbacks:

  • Requires downtime, which may not be acceptable for production environments.
  • No HA capabilities during the upgrade.
  • All traffic is temporarily disrupted during the procedure. 

Choosing Between Rolling Upgrade and Out-of-Service Upgrade

CriteriaRolling UpgradeOut-of-Service Upgrade
DowntimeMinimal or none (traffic continues to flow as much as it can, controller failvoer will interrupt the traffic briefly).Requires complete downtime during upgrade.
ComplexityModerate (HA configurations).Low (direct and straightforward process).
Upgrade SpeedSlower (sequential upgrade of nodes/modules).Faster (entire system upgraded at once).
Use CaseCritical, production systems with HA.Non-critical systems or planned maintenance windows.
RiskLower risk (traffic is always handled).Higher risk (full outage if something goes wrong).
