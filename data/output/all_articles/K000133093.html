K000133093: How does BIG-IP select pool members using OneConnect for L7 requests

Type
Support Solution
Published
2023-03-30
Updated
2025-04-02
Applies to
  • BIG-IP
  • BIG-IP LTM
Source
https://my.f5.com/manage/s/article/K000133093

Description

This article explains how the BIG-IP will make load-balancing decisions for your L7 HTTP requests when using HTTP and OneConnect profiles. There could be two conditions when it needs to choose a connection to send the requests.

  1. When your pool members have no connections, for example, when you just finished creating the configuration and started serving traffic. Note, there is no connection for each pool member you configured, not just no connections on your pool. For this scenario, the BIG-IP will create a new connection(using your LB method configured on Pool) for each of the pool members and use the connection one by one.
For example, for pool members A, B, C, D, and E. Initially, member A, B, C, D, and E has no connection. At some moment, 5 requests come sequentially. It will create a new connection for each of the members of A, B, C, D, and E respectively, even if there is an idle connection to reuse.
 
  1. When each of your pool members has at least one connection. The BIG-IP selects one free connection to reuse from all qualified connections in a round-robin manner.
For example, for pool members A, B, C, D, and E. Assume any of them already has one connection. At some moment, 5 requests come sequentially. It will reuse those qualified idle connections one by one starting from any of the A, B, C, D, and E.
 

Environment

  • BIG-IP LTM
  • HTTP
  • OneConnect

Cause

This is informational.

Recommended Actions

Understanding how the BIG-IP makes this decision would help troubleshooting scenarios, for example, imbalance situations on L4 or/and L7 levels. The load-balance between your pool members may be influenced by how your pool member process connections. If further troubleshooting is required, you may consider collecting tmctl stats(refer to the below command), pool stats, connections table, traffic capture, and qkview.

For collecting tmctl stats, you may use the below command to collect it periodly:

tmctl pool_member_stat pool_name=<pool_name>

For example, if you have pool example_pool in partition Common:

tmctl pool_member_stat pool_name=/Common/example_pool

 

Additional Information

When your connection becomes idle and finally reaches the idle timeout value configured in the TCP profile, the system may not close the connection immediately. For example, if you configure it to timeout in 60 seconds, it might close the idle connection after 60 seconds(eg. 65 seconds) after it becomes idle.
