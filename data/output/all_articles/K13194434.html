Description

Cleaning optical transceivers and cables provided by F5

You should consider using this article if you want to clean 1G, 10G, 40G, or 100G single-mode LC cables or multi-mode multiple-fiber push-on/pull-off (MPO) cables and transceivers provided by or purchased from F5.

Dust caps and protectors

While dust caps protect the ferrule ends of optical cables and the receptacles of optical transceivers, they can become dirty and a source of contamination for both transceivers and optical cables. Make sure that when you remove them to place them in a clean container, sealed to limit possible contamination until you need them again. Whether or not you reuse the dust caps, take care to make sure they remain as free of contamination as possible. Always use dust caps on optical cables or optical transceivers when they are unplugged and not in use.

MPO cables

  1. MPO cables are multi-fiber cables, typically with blue and black connectors. MTP is a brand name referring to cables that have some minor tweaks but interconnect with MPO cables.
  2. MPO cables come in many different fiber counts, and F5 currently only sells the 24-fiber cables.
  3. All of the MPO cables F5 sells are also multi-mode OM3 cables, which is the standard for F5 optical transceivers.

Prerequisites

This article applies to cleaning the following optical transceivers and cable types:

  • 1.25 GbE SFP single-mode and multi-mode LC
  • 10 GbE SFP+ single-mode and multi-mode LC
  • 10 GbE XFP single-mode and multi-mode LC
  • 40 GbE QSFP+ multi-mode MPO and single-mode LC
  • 100 GbE QSFP28 multi-mode MPO and single-mode LC

Required supplies

  • Fiber optic connection cleaner or lint-free wipes
  • An MPO or LC cleaner (click-pen)
  • 99 percent Isopropyl rubbing alcohol
  • (Optional but recommended) A microscope for use with optics

General warnings and recommendations

  • Always turn off any laser sources before inspecting optical components.
  • Always make sure cables and transceivers are disconnected prior to cleaning them.
  • Always inspect ferrule ends and optics before cleaning.
  • Always clean optical cables before plugging them in.
  • If required, make sure to wear appropriate safety glasses.
  • Never use wet cleaning unless you have a way to remove the alcohol and residue.
  • Never touch the end face of optical cable ferrules.
  • Never reuse any tissue fabric that was used in a previous cleaning.
  • Never touch the clean area of a cleaning fabric.
  • Never allow cleaning alcohol to evaporate from the optical cable ferrule because it leaves residue that is difficult to remove.

Procedures

  • Wet cleaning optical cable ends (both MPO and LC)
  • Dry cleaning (light debris) LC transceivers (1G, 10G, 40G LR, 100G LR)
  • Wet cleaning (heavy debris and oil) LC transceivers (1G, 10G, 40G LR, 100G LR)
  • Dry Cleaning MPO Transceivers (40G SR, 100G SR)

Wet cleaning optical cable ends (both MPO and LC)

  1. Pull out the end of the fabric on the fiber optic connection cleaner to get a new piece in the cleaner window.
  2. Put a few drops of alcohol on the end of the fabric that was just pulled out creating a half-wet area.

    Figure: Apply alcohol to fabric

  3. Wipe the end of the fiber optic cable/connector in a circle on the wet end of the cleaner fabric.

    Figure: Wipe the end of the fiber

  4. Wipe the end of the fiber optic cable/connector multiple times on the dry end. Your goal is to wipe off any oil and contaminates on the wet side, then remove the alcohol on the dry side.
  5. Open the cap on the MPO click-pen cleaner and insert the fiber optic cable end into it.
  6. Push the cleaner in to the cable end until it clicks. This step helps remove any remaining alcohol, as well as removes any lint fibers left on the cable end.

    Figure: Push the cleaner into the cable end until it clicks

  7. (Optional but recommended) Check with a microscope to see that all the alcohol and debris has been removed.

    Figure: Clean connections viewed through microsocope

Note: F5 recommends cleaning cable ends every time you use new cables. Doing so can help keep from transferring debris into the optical transceivers.

Note: Never reuse a lint-free wipe or the same spot on the connection cleaner because doing so can recontaminate an otherwise clean optical cable.

Dry cleaning (light debris) LC transceivers (1G, 10G, 40G LR, 100G LR)

Figure: 10g SR, 1g SR, 10g SR, 100g LR optics

Note: You can clean LC transceivers with click pens or canned air.

  1. Blow a few short bursts of canned air into the transceiver.
  2. Insert the click pen into the transceiver and click it twice.

Wet cleaning (heavy debris and oil) LC transceivers (1G, 10G, 40G LR, 100G LR)

Figure: Examples of clean and dirty fiber

  1. Dip a click-pen into clean, dry alcohol (old alcohol has absorbed water, which does not dry up quickly).
  2. Insert the click pen into the transceiver and click several times.
  3. Use a few short bursts of canned air to blow out the remaining alcohol.

    Figure: Compressed air duster

  4. Insert a dry click pen into the transceiver and click several times.
  5. (Optional but recommended) Check with a microscope to see that all the alcohol and debris has been removed.
  6. Repeat steps 1-5 as needed until the optic is clean.

Dry Cleaning MPO Transceivers (40G SR, 100G SR)

Warning: F5 provides 100G SR MPO optical transceivers that contain a metal screen inside of them that does not react well to cleaning supplies. If you use a click pen on a 100G MPO optical transceiver you risk pushing debris behind the screen, which may render the optic unusable and irreparable.

  1. For the 40G/100G SR optical transceivers (MPO), only use a canned air duster.
  2. Blow a few short bursts of canned air into the transceiver from the canned air duster. This usually blows out the dust and leaves the optic in usable condition.
