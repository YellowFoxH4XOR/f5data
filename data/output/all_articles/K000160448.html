Description

This article is for you who are trying to do basic troubleshooting/investigation into what is happening on your BIG-IP. In order to do this you will have to upload a QKView to iHealth.

After generating a QKView file (also known as a "support snapshot" when you generate it in the configuration utility), you may upload it to ihealth.

WHY SHOULD YOU USE IHEALTH:
For proactive or reactive reasons:
Proactive: You want to be familiar with your BIG-IP default behavior and apply recommended practices before an issue occurs (see the section about Diagnostics further down)
Reactive: iHealth is a huge help for troubleshooting most issues, it is an easy way to go through the logs.
Note: The BIG-IP is not a logging machine, although it enables your to search for logs, there are better options out there (you can use your favorite search engine and look search for "SIEM").

iHealth is a platform for analyzing your BIG-IP, the resources, configuration and logs.
Note: Logs might be truncated, hence we recommend you to generate the qkview right after the issue occurs (if there is one), and enable the "Unlimited snaplen" option, that would be the "-s0" flag via the CLI.

For more information on how to generate a QKView file, see "Related Content" section at the bottom of this article.

Environment

BIG-IP, all versions

Cause

None

Answer/Recommended Actions

The iHealth login

The iHealth page can be found HERE, or you can copy paste the URL: https://account.f5.com/ihealth2

Login, or Sign up (You just need an email, nothing else)

 

The iHealth startpage

From the iHealth startpage you can upload qkviews.

When you upload a qkview to iHealth, we recommend adding the case number (if you are trying to add multiple cases to the qkview they should be delimited by either spaces or commas). If you have a case open, we strongly recommend case number.

After the uploading the QKView, it might take another few minutes for iHealth to extract the logs from the compressed data in the QKView... give it a few minutes.

 

After it has finished loading you can now press the uploaded QKView file

 

When pressing the loaded QKView file, you will enter the Status page.

The QKView

From here you have an overview of the BIG-IP. Within the red box you can see the system identity information, like what kind of platform the BIG-IP is running on, the hostname, timezone, uptime and basic resource specification.

There is the "Commands" tab, where you have access to basic commands that can be run on the BIG-IP. (press the "Expand/Collapse All" button to see everything)

Next we have the "Graphs" tab, from here you can see basic graphs (not that these are based on 5 minute average).

Next, we have one of the primary tools for diagnosing your device, the "Diagnostics" tab. From here you can see an overview of notifications, their severity, (in many cases) an article on how to resolve the issue, details (press "Details") and even some CVE's to that might affect your BIG-IP and how to resolve them. The filtering function can help you focus your attention on relevant notifications... try it out.

Next we have the "FIles" tab, from here you can search specific folders for specific strings (you can even use regular expressions). 

Note: The files you can see here are NOT all the files on the BIG-IP. Some files could be missing due to truncation, transformation, compression or priority during the creation of the QKView.

Due to the log rotation on the BIG-IP, many logs are not available after 7 - 8 days.

Finally (in this article) we have the "Log Search" tab, from here you can search from specific strings that might appear in the logs. Here you can filter by log-level, which files the logs appear in, date, also you can choose what files to focus on or hide.

You also have the opportunity to export the logs.

 

If you have any further questions, feel free to contact the F5 support.
