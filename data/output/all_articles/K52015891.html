Issue

You should consider using these procedures under the following conditions:

  • The BIG-IP system does not turn on.
  • The BIG-IP system shows that a power supply is not functioning.

Symptoms

As a result of a power supply issue, you may encounter the following symptoms:

  • You observe power supply error messages.
  • The system experiences loss of device management.
  • The system experiences loss of network connectivity.

Recommended Actions

When dealing with a possible power supply issue and before troubleshooting a power-related problem, you should review the following platform-specific documents:

  • K000139244: Reseating Power Supply Units (PSUs) in F5 rSeries and VELOS Devices
  • K36588206: AC power supply units for iSeries and rSeries platform models should not be interchanged
  • K14288: The BIG-IP 2000 and 4000 series platforms may generate power supply read failure error messages
  • K14636: The BIG-IP 5000/7000 series platforms may generate power supply read failure error messages
  • K14702: The BIG-IP 10000 series platforms may generate power supply read failure error messages
  • K14695: Error Message: 010d0006:0: Chassis power supply <X> has experienced an issue

Procedures

When your system experiences power supply issues, you can use the following troubleshooting steps:

  • Verify the power supply status from tmsh
  • Troubleshoot a device with a single power supply that no longer turns on
  • Troubleshoot a device with multiple power supplies that is generating power supply-related error messages
  • Troubleshoot a device with multiple power supplies that no longer turn on

Verify the power supply status from tmsh

This procedure shows you how to check the power supply status from the TMOS Shell (tmsh).

Note: You cannot check the power supply status using the Configuration utility.

If tmsh is still accessible on your system, perform the following steps:

Impact of procedure: Performing the following procedure should not have a negative impact on your system.

  1. Log in to tmsh by entering the following command:

    tmsh

  2. To display the current chassis power supply status, enter the following command:

    show /sys hardware

    tmsh automatically directs the output to the less pager utility and you should see the Chassis Power Supply Status section on the first page.

    For example:

    Chassis Power Supply Status
      Index  Status  Current
      1      up      AC
      2      down    AC

  3. Press the space bar to page through the rest of the output or enter q to quit out of less.

Troubleshoot a device with a single power supply that no longer turns on

If your system does not have redundant power supplies and no longer turns on, perform the following steps:

Impact of procedure: Performing the following procedure should not have a negative impact on your system.

  1. Check the power supply for signs of damage.
    1. Check for front LCD panel for LED activity.
    2. Check the power supply for any signs of arcing or sparks.
    3. Check for a smoky or burnt odor.

    Important: If you think the power supply has caught fire at some point, contact F5 Support immediately to report the issue.

  2. Check the power cable.
    1. Ensure that the power cable is fully seated.
    2. Check for signs of wire damage.
    3. Try other power supply cables.
  3. Verify your power source.
    1. Plug the power cable into different outlets that you know are supplying the proper voltage.
    2. Verify circuit breaker is not overloaded.
    3. Verify fused power outlet for a blown fuse or breaker integrated power outlet in triggered state.

Troubleshoot a device with multiple power supplies that is generating power supply-related error messages

Important: Although power supplies may appear to have the same size and shape, they may be incompatible between F5 devices. You must ensure that power supplies are only swapped between the same platform model. For more information, refer to: K36588206: AC power supply units for iSeries and rSeries platform models should not be interchanged.

If your BIG-IP system has redundant power supplies and you observe error messages that reference possible power supply issues, perform the following steps:

Impact of procedure: Performing the following procedure should not have a negative impact on your system.

  1. Review error messages for the affected power supply.

    An error message may appear in the /var/log/ltm file similar to the following:

    Chassis power supply 2 has experienced an issue. Status is as follows: FAN=bad; STATUS=bad.

  2. Check the power supply for signs of damage.
    1. Check for LED activity on the power supply slot.
    2. Check the power supply for signs of arcing or sparks.
    3. Check for a smoky or burnt odor.

    Important: If you think the power supply has caught fire at some point, contact F5 Support immediately to report the issue.

  3. Check the power cable.
    1. Ensure that the power cable is fully seated.
    2. Check for signs of wire damage.
    3. Try other power supply cables.
  4. Ensure the power supply is fully seated.
    1. Disconnect the AC power cord or DC terminal block from the power supply.
    2. Loosen the power supply screw by turning it counterclockwise with an appropriate screwdriver, if necessary.
    3. Grasp the ejector handle and rotate it downward to eject the power supply from the system.
    4. Remove the power supply from the system by pulling straight towards yourself.
    5. Ensure that the latch on the power supply is in the down position and slide the power supply into the power supply slot until the latch engages.
    6. Rotate the latch upward to fully seat the power supply.
    7. Tighten the screw into place.
    8. Attach the AC power cord or DC terminal block to the power supply.
    9. For AC power supplies, ensure that the power switch, located next to the screw, is in the ON position.
  5. Verify your power source.
    1. Plug the power cable into different outlets that you know are supplying the proper voltage.

      Note: In rare instances, certain BIG-IP platforms may erroneously generate power supply error messages that indicate zero milli-voltage. Replacing the power supply or BIG-IP system will not correct this issue. For more information, refer to K17356: Certain BIG-IP platforms using DC power supplies may erroneously generate a power supply error message indicating zero milli-voltage.

Troubleshoot a device with multiple power supplies that no longer turn on

If your BIG-IP system has redundant power supplies and is no longer turning on, perform the following steps:

Impact of procedure: Performing the following procedure should not have a negative impact on your system.

  1. Check the power supplies for signs of damage.

    Important: If you think the power supply has caught fire at some point, contact F5 Support immediately to report the issue.

    1. Check the front panel for LED activity.
    2. Check the power supplies for signs of arcing or sparks.
    3. Check for a smoky or burnt odor.
  2. Check the power cables.
    1. Ensure power cables are fully seated.
    2. Check for signs of wire damage.
    3. Try other power supply cables.
  3. Ensure that the power supplies are fully seated.
    1. Disconnect the AC power cord or DC terminal block from the power supply.
    2. Loosen the power supply screw by turning it counterclockwise with an appropriate screwdriver, if necessary.
    3. Grasp the ejector handle and rotate it downward to eject the power supply from the system.
    4. Remove the power supply from the system by pulling straight towards yourself.
    5. Ensure that the latch on the power supply is in the down position and slide the power supply into the power supply slot until the latch engages.
    6. Rotate the latch upward to fully seat the power supply.
    7. Tighten the screw into place.
    8. Attach the AC power cord or DC terminal block to the power supply.
    9. For AC power supplies, ensure that the power switch, located next to the screw, is in the ON position.
  4. Verify your power source.
    1. Plug the power cable into different outlets that you know are supplying the proper voltage.
    2. Verify circuit breaker is not overloaded.
    3. Verify fused power outlet for a blown fuse or breaker integrated power outlet in triggered state.
  5. Swap out power supplies.
    1. If you have another system of the same model with working, replaceable, power supplies, try one of those power supplies in each power supply slot on the system that is not turning on.
