K000135030: ARP probes towards rSeries management IP answered by multiple MAC addresses

Description

Observing intermittent management connectivity towards rSeries appliance.

Packet capture reveals that the management interface is responding to ARPs from multiple MAC addresses.

For example:

00:00:5E:00:53:01  ff:ff:ff:ff:ff:ff  Who has 10.10.10.1? (ARP Probe)
14:a9:d0:18:db:03  00:00:5E:00:53:01  10.10.10.1 is at 14:a9:d0:18:db:03
14:a9:d0:18:db:fe  00:00:5E:00:53:01  10.10.10.1 is at 14:a9:d0:18:db:fe (duplicate use of 10.10.10.1 detected!)

In this example, 10.10.10.1 responds from both 14:a9:d0:18:db:03 and 14:a9:d0:18:db:fe.

Environment

  • rSeries
  • Management access
  • ARP

Cause

Bug ID 1295657

ARPs sent to the system are ARP probes, with a sender IP address of 0.0.0.0. ARP probes are used by a node that intends to use an IP, to determine if that IP is in use.

In this situation, the ARP probe is answered by the mgmt interface, and the mgmt0-system macvlan sub-interface.

When an external systems sends packets to the mgmt MAC (not the mgmt0-system MAC), they will be dropped. This causes intermittent connectivity issues.

Recommended Actions

If you cannot change the way the ARPs are being sent to the system, a workaround is to update these ARP-related kernel parameters:

sysctl -w net.ipv4.conf.mgmt.arp_ignore=1
sysctl -w net.ipv4.conf.mgmt.arp_announce=2
sysctl -w net.ipv4.conf.mgmt.rp_filter=1

Once you have confirmed that these changes have no adverse impact in your environment, you can apply them persistently:

cat << EOF > /etc/sysctl.d/mgmt-arp.conf
net.ipv4.conf.mgmt.arp_ignore = 1
net.ipv4.conf.mgmt.arp_announce = 2
net.ipv4.conf.mgmt.rp_filter = 1
EOF

sysctl -p /etc/sysctl.d/mgmt-arp.conf
