Known Issue

Unsolicited neighbor advertisements are not propagated through a Virtual Local Area Network (VLAN) group.

This issue occurs when the following condition is met:

  • VLAN groups are configured for VLANs that are also configured for IPv6.

The BIG-IP system does not forward unsolicited neighbor advertisement messages to all VLANs, even if messages are not destined for the BIG-IP system. This behavior may adversely affect mechanisms, such as duplicate address detection within IPv6. Neighbor advertisement messages are required from IPv6 nodes in response to neighbor solicitation messages. However, a node may elect to send unsolicited advertisement messages to propagate new information quickly. For more information, refer to the RFC 2461 section 7.2.6: Sending Unsolicited Neighbor Advertisements.

Note: This link takes you to a resource outside of AskF5. The third party could remove the document without our knowledge.

Impact

Mechanisms that rely on unsolicited neighbor advertisement messages does not perform as expected.

Symptoms

As a result of this issue, you may encounter the following symptom:

  • Duplicate IPv6 addresses on two VLANs in a VLAN group exist without detection.

Workaround

To work around this issue, you should not use VLAN groups for VLANs with IPv6 traffic.
