Known Issue

Virtual Local Area Networks (VLAN) groups with EtherIP tunnel members may drop packets

This issue occurs when all of the following conditions are met:

  • The BIG-IP system is configured with VLAN groups that have both a VLAN and EtherIP tunnel as members.
  • The BIG-IP system attempts to bridge packets with sizes exceeding the effective MTU of the EtherIP tunnel.

The BIG-IP system may drop packets with sizes exceeding the effective maximum transmission unit (MTU) of the EtherIP tunnel. The effective MTU size is computed by subtracting the encapsulation overhead of 36 bytes from the configured MTU size of the underlying VLAN. The underlying VLAN of the tunnel is the VLAN used to transmit tunnel packets.

Impact

Traffic is negatively impacted while packets destined for the EtherIP tunnel are dropped by the BIG-IP system

Symptoms

As a result of this issue, you may encounter one or more of the following symptoms:

  • Network latency or abnormal packet loss is observed for traffic traversing EtherIP tunnels.
  • Packets needing fragmentation are dropped by the BIG-IP system without sending an ICMP Destination Unreachable message.
  • You are able to ping across the tunnel but application traffic is intermittently affected.

Workaround

To work around this issue, you can increase the configurable MTU size of the VLAN underlying the EtherIP tunnel to account for the encapsulation overhead of 36 bytes. This avoids the issue and will additionally avoid fragmentation of encapsulated packets.
