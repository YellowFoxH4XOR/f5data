K000151699: Client doesn't Receive the TLS Segment of a Reassembled PDU

Type
Support Solution
Published
2025-06-26
Updated
2026-06-24
Applies to
  • BIG-IP
  • BIG-IP LTM: 17.1.1
Source
https://my.f5.com/manage/s/article/K000151699

Description

Intermittent session timeouts, HTTP GET timeouts, or dropped connections may occur. Simultaneous packet captures on BIG-IP and Client can reveal that clients do not receive the expected TLS segment of a reassembled PDU, causing the client to drop the session, however capture from the BIG-IP shows it's sending and even retransmitting. These issues are more pronounced under high load, especially when backend servers respond rapidly and in bursts. No specific error messages are logged on BIG-IP; packet analysis may show missing or out-of-order packets from the client’s perspective.

Environment

  • BIG-IP LTM VE
  • Deployed on OpenStack (Red Hat), HP hardware
  • Network: SR-IOV enabled
  • High traffic/load scenarios without intermediate proxies
  • TCP profiles tested: Standard, WAN, Progressive

Cause

Insufficient RX/TX queue sizes on the BIG-IP VE’s virtual network interfaces cause dropped packets during high throughput or bursty traffic conditions. This results in incomplete or out-of-order packet delivery to clients and leads to session timeouts and connection drops. The use of SR-IOV and high-speed backend responses can overwhelm default queue settings. You can use the below command to verify if the queue drops are increasing:

tmctl -d /var/tmstat/blade tmm/iq_rx_stats -OK dropped -L 5

tmctl -d /var/tmstat/blade tmm/iq_tx_stats -OK dropped -L 5

Recommended Actions

Adjust RX and TX queue sizes for BIG-IP VE network interfaces to handle higher throughput and bursty traffic patterns. You can follow the procedure given in K35493303 to modify the queue sizes.

Additional Information

  • Increased queue settings persist across reboots and upgrades since they are stored in /config.
  • Changing TCP profiles (e.g., f5-tcp-wan or f5-tcp-progressive) may reduce error rates but is not a guaranteed solution and could introduce other issues.
  • If problems persist, attempt packet capture on an intermediate device between BIG-IP and client to further isolate where loss occurs.
  • Ensure backend servers are properly resourced and tuned for expected load patterns.
