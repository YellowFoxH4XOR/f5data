Description

In a BIG-IP LTM High Availability (HA) environment, health monitor traffic sent through a GRE tunnel only succeeds on the Active unit and fails on the Standby unit. This occurs when the GRE tunnel is configured with a floating self-IP. After failover, the GRE tunnel becomes active on the new Active unit, but health monitoring remains non-functional on the Standby. The issue can impact HA-group failover logic and monitoring reliability.

Environment

  • BIG-IP LTM version 17.5.1
  • HA (Active/Standby) configuration
  • GRE tunnels configured for traffic between BIG-IP devices and remote endpoints
  • GRE tunnel initially configured with a floating self-IP (traffic-group-1)
  • Health monitors targeting devices behind the GRE tunnel

Cause

The GRE tunnel configured with a floating self-IP is only present on the Active unit. The Standby unit does not have the floating self-IP, so it cannot send or receive GRE traffic for monitoring purposes.

Answer/Recommended Actions

  • For health monitoring to function on both Active and Standby units, configure a GRE tunnel on each device using its own non-floating self-IP for monitoring traffic, rather than a floating self-IP.
  • This ensures that each unit can independently send and receive health monitor traffic through its dedicated GRE tunnel, regardless of HA status.

Additional Information

  • After configuring GRE tunnels on non-floating self-IPs, health monitoring works on both units and GRE traffic flows after failover.
