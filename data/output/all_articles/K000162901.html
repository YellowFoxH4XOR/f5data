Topic

F5 shipped its first hardened release (HR1) on July 15, 2026. It addresses a broad range of security issues, including critical vulnerabilities, and should be deployed as soon as possible. We are also making two changes to our approach going forward: hardened releases are moving to a six-week cadence and we are pausing regular security notifications.

What we're asking you to do:

  • Deploy hardened releases as soon as possible. HR1 fixes a broad range of security issues, including critical vulnerabilities in both the control plane and the data plane.
  • Subscribe to F5 security announcements or forward this email to an appropriate contact so your organization is notified of future releases.

Description

On July 15, 2026, F5 shipped its first hardened release (HR1) under the cadence we announced earlier this summer. Hardened releases are security-only updates that do not contain new features or functionality. This scope limits the change surface, simplifies testing and validation, and allows customers to close critical security gaps while minimizing disruption to existing deployments.

In the weeks since, we have gathered feedback from customers and continued to assess how the threat landscape is developing. Based on what we have learned, we are making two important changes to our approach.

  • What has changed and why
  • Why deploying hardened releases matters
  • What we're asking you to do

What has changed and why

  • Hardened releases will move to a six-week cadence. We originally planned to ship hardened releases on the third Wednesday of every month. We are moving to a six-week cadence, with the next hardened release (HR2) available on September 2. Customer feedback following HR1 indicated that a monthly cadence does not provide sufficient time for most organizations to test, stage, and deploy — particularly as customers absorb more frequent releases from vendors across their entire technology stack. A six-week cadence provides additional time to plan and execute without materially slowing the delivery of fixes.
  • We will pause regular security notifications. We previously announced monthly security notifications disclosing details of the vulnerabilities fixed in the prior hardened release, assuming a 30-day delay would give customers sufficient time to update. Based on discussions with customers and others across the industry, we have revised that assessment. Advanced frontier AI models continue to improve at turning limited vulnerability information into working exploits, which means publishing details in security advisories about what we’ve fixed creates immediate risk for systems that have not yet been updated.

We will retain flexibility to adjust timing, content, or notification approach where regulatory, contractual, coordinated-disclosure obligations, government coordination, active exploitation, materiality assessment, embargo, or customer-protection considerations require a different approach.

The landscape is evolving quickly and established practices are being reassessed across the industry. We implemented a monthly cadence with delayed disclosure based on our best assessment at the time. After one release cycle, it is clear that adjustments are warranted. Our number one priority is protecting our customers and ensuring you have the necessary time to update your F5 systems. We will continue to evaluate our approach and adapt as needed.

For more information, please refer to Our first hardened release cycle: What we learned and how we're changing.

Why deploying hardened releases matters

We recognize that many organizations assess risk and prioritize remediation based on the severity and impact of individual vulnerabilities.

Today, attackers are increasingly chaining lower-severity vulnerabilities together into novel exploits. This means the severity rating of any single fix is a less reliable guide to the true importance of a release. We ask you to treat every hardened release as though it closes a critical gap, and to prioritize deployment accordingly.

HR1 fixes a broad range of issues, including critical vulnerabilities in both the control plane and the data plane. We expect this will be the case for all hardened releases in the near future. A longer cadence provides additional time to plan but it does not reduce the urgency of deploying available fixes.

Moving to a faster cadence has gone hand in hand with scaling how we validate every release. We have expanded our quality assurance capacity and AI-assisted test generation across platform functionality, upgrade paths, and critical workflows. This expanded validation is designed to reduce the risk of regressions while maintaining the pace of delivery.

What we're asking you to do

  • Subscribe to F5 security announcements so you are notified of future releases.
  • Deploy hardened releases as soon as possible. Treat these as critical updates, given the nature of the security issues they address.

Protecting our customers is our highest priority and the basis for these decisions. We recognize this is a significant adjustment, and we are committed to supporting you.

If you are not the appropriate contact for this update, please forward this to your security or infrastructure team and ask them to subscribe to F5 security announcements. Contact your account team if you have questions, or F5 Support if you need help planning your upgrade.

Thank you for your partnership and for the trust you place in F5.
