  • Any other filters for traffic they would like to have assessed

Troubleshooting Routing and Latency Issues

  • Routing Issues – defined as problems involving suboptimal routing, loops, unintended upstream issues or configuration, or network deficiencies leading to increased delays 

  • Latency Issues – defined as high delays (RTT), jitter, packet loss, or other performance degradations affecting customer applications and unexpected regional delays 

 

  • All Customer requests will be assessed and prioritized based on severity and business impact  

 

Issue Validation: 

  • Confirm the routing or latency issue by using customer-provided data, including traceroutes, ping, timestamps, HAR files, logs)
  • Run diagnostics using internal tools, attempt to replicate the issue from similar regions where the issue is observed 

Communication 

  • Maintain customer communication, gather issue details
  • Set expectations for next steps and provide updates towards resolution based on Engineering feedback 

Customer Communication 

  • Support teams maintain customer communication and provide updates as investigation progresses, at least every 72hrs or earlier if applicable
  • Support teams communicate resolution, validate with customer to ensure expectations are met 

Creating a Support case via Console including all necessary details for triage:

  • Impacted traffic: Source and destination IPs, ports, timestamps, region(s)
  • Evidence: traceroutes, packet captures, HAR files, logs, latency benchmarks
  • Customer impact: description of how the issue reported affects the business 
Service Policy Configuration

Process and procedure for assisting customers in creating, managing, and troubleshooting service policies within the F5 Distributed Cloud (XC) Console. It highlights documentation and tools that can aid customers in using service policies effectively. The originator of this request is the customer, and the question arises when they want to configure or use service policies to define security rules for traffic control and access management. 

Support for standard customers includes providing the initial response, claiming ownership of cases, triaging the issue, and following the technical escalation process as required. Assistance is focused on addressing break/fix scenarios related to Service Policies by providing troubleshooting and documentation. However, support does not extend to creating or modifying Service Policies; instead, guidance is offered for resolving issues within the scope of existing configurations. 

The customer must have an active F5 Distributed Cloud deployment and appropriate access to the F5 Distributed Cloud Console. Support will require relevant details about the customer's service policy requirements, such as traffic rules, access control criteria, and any error details for troubleshooting. The process does not apply to scenarios outside the scope of Service Policies, such as issues related to underlying infrastructure, unrelated configuration settings, or feature requests beyond the customer's current support tier.
WAF Allowlist/Denylist Configuration

For customers who experience issues related to Allowlist/Denylist and and Service Policy configurations misconfigurations.

SOC will troubleshoot the request and provide solutions on to how to resolve the issue.

The customer must have an active F5 Distributed Cloud deployment and appropriate access to the F5 Distributed Cloud Console. Support will require relevant details about the customer's service policy requirements, and any error details for troubleshooting. 
Load Balancer configuration assistance 

Request to create or modify a Load Balancer configuration, including domains, certificates, origin pools, routing, health checks, or related settings. 

Validate request scope, gather missing details, assess impact and dependencies, coordinate an approved change window, follow internal change management procedures, implement or guide configuration changes, document risk and rollback plans, maintain communication, and complete post-change validation. 

Provide complete configuration requirements, confirm expected post-change behavior, supply certificate details when applicable, approve execution timing, and validate the service after the change is completed. Missing or incomplete configuration details require clarification before implementation; emergency production changes must still follow expedited change management procedures.
Ad Hoc Incident resolutions 

Customer-initiated support requests for solving incident impacting production applications

Collect crucial information first: 

  • Exact start time and first symptom observed  
  • Affected apps/namespace/LBFQDNs/VIPs and regions  
  • Actual error code or screenshot seen by the customer. 

 

Categorize service affected: DNS/LB/WAF/Routing/Origin health/Origin Error issue. 
 

Customer must create a Support case through the Console and provide the necessary details for triage, including impacted application or service, namespace, load balancer FQDNs/VIPs, affected regions, exact start time, first observed symptoms, current business impact, error codes, screenshots, logs, packet captures, recent changes, and any troubleshooting already completed.

TaskDescriptionScopeCustomer ExpectationsExceptions
Proactive notifications for observed attacks SOC proactively notifies the customer when a potential DDoS attack is observed against their origin IP address or IP subnet and coordinates next steps through a support case. 

Monitor and analyze triggered alerts for potential DDoS activity. 

Determine whether each alert is a false positive, false negative, or confirmed attack. 

Create a proactive customer notification through a support case when an attack is identified. 

Review and respond to customer updates in the support ticket. 

Complete any required follow-up actions while adhering to internal change management policies. 

Review the proactive notification sent through the support case. 

Confirm whether the observed traffic aligns with expected activity or requires mitigation. 

Provide any required direction, approval, or implementation requirements through the support ticket. 

If the alert is determined to be a false positive, no proactive attack notification is required unless follow-up is needed for customer awareness. 

If the analysis identifies a false negative or confirmed attack, the DDoS Analyst should proceed with the appropriate notification and support case handling. 

If customer-requested actions require changes, the team must follow internal change management policies before implementation. 

Response to customer inquiries on attack traffic parameters Request for additional details about a DDoS attack or event through a new or existing support case.Review the event and available attack traffic data, determine whether F5 has enough information to address the request, investigate the customer’s questions, and communicate findings back through the support case. Open or update a support case with clear expectations, specific questions, and any required details needed for SOC/Support to investigate the request. F5 may not be able to provide information that is unavailable in the event data, including attribution of who was responsible for the attack; responses are limited to details F5 can validate from known attack or event data.
Monitoring of Layer 7 attacks for any DDOS related vectors and any collateral damage F5 monitors Layer 7 DDoS alerts and potential collateral damage to help identify customer-impacting activity and initiate proactive support when needed. Monitor and investigate Layer 7 alerts using telemetry and logs, validate whether the activity is a DDoS event or collateral impact, engage Engineering when additional help is required, create a case for affected customers, and provide customer updates according to the required cadence. Create a Support case through the Console when reporting an issue and include all details needed for triage, such as impacted applications, observed symptoms, timing, scope of impact, and any relevant traffic or error information. Proactive case creation applies when SOC identifies significant risk, confirmed impact, or suspected collateral damage affecting one or more customers.
Development of Real-Time Incident Procedures (RTIP) specific to customer SOPs (Standard Operation Procedures)Develop and maintain a customer-specific RTIP runbook so F5 XC DDoS protection follows the customer’s documented SOPs during an observed DDoS attack. Initiate the RTIP development process through a customer support case, review the customer-specific RTIP when an attack is identified, and follow the notes documented in the profile page. Provide the relevant SOP details and operational expectations needed to create or update the RTIP, including any customer-specific response requirements for protected IP addresses or subnets.The document does not define explicit exception scenarios; if no RTIP exists, the RTIP is incomplete, or the attack scenario falls outside the documented notes, the case should be reviewed through the appropriate support escalation path before taking non-standard action. 
Validation of attack traffic mitigation stages  Validate that attack traffic is detected and mitigated appropriately after a customer support case is opened.Investigate the reported attack traffic, validate detection and mitigation stages, apply the appropriate DDoS mitigation strategy, communicate findings, and recommend next steps. Open a customer support case and provide the information needed for the Support Analyst to investigate and validate the attack detection and mitigation process. Any deviations or special handling should follow the applicable support escalation process. 
Ad Hoc generation of firewall rules Request ad hoc firewall rule creation or updates through a Support case to address a specific customer need. Review the customer case, confirm requirements and account details, follow internal change management processes, implement the requested firewall configuration, validate deployment, document actions taken in the case, and notify the customer when complete. Open a Support case, provide clear expectations and firewall rule requirements, and ensure an F5 Gateway is configured before implementation. Requests may require additional clarification, may be delayed or adjusted if requirements are incomplete, may depend on SOC Engineer shift coverage, and must still meet internal change management policies and validation requirements before deployment is confirmed. 
    Daily/Weekly/Monthly reports on alerts received for selected time period and mitigation actions taken Provide the customer with a formal DDoS metrics and threat profile report for a defined reporting period, typically as part of a QBR or authorized account review. SOC may be consulted by the Account Manager, or CSM, to clarify data, provide insight, and support interpretation of DDoS reporting information. The customer has no required actions during report creation and is informed only when the completed report is delivered. The process is not customer-triggered or available on demand. Exceptions may occur when an Account Manager, CSM, or account team member authorizes an on-demand report, including reporting for a specific attack or customer agreement-driven need.
