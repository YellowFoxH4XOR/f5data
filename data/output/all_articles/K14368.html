Known Issue

A VLAN failsafe event may not trigger a failover in high-availability (HA) vCMP guest pairs.

This issue occurs when all of the following conditions are met:

  • Two vCMP guests are configured in an HA group between two VIPRION chassis.
  • VLAN failsafe is configured on at least one VLAN associated with an interface.
  • MAC masquerading is configured for the traffic group.
  • At least one BIG-IP LTM object (SNAT, NAT, virtual server, etc.) is configured.
  • The vCMP guest transitions from active to standby, and then back to active.

After a failover and failback scenario, the active vCMP guest has the translation address of a BIG-IP LTM object listed in its neighbor table with its own MAC address. During a subsequent network-traffic-loss event on the VLAN, the vCMP guest sends a probe to itself due to the SNAT translation address and MAC address being listed in the neighbor table.

Impact

The vCMP guest may not fail over as expected during a VLAN failsafe event.

Symptoms

As a result of this issue, you may encounter the following symptoms:

  • A local traffic object is listed in the active vCMP guest's neighbor table with its own MAC address.
  • The vCMP guest sends VLAN failsafe probes to itself.

Workaround

To temporarily recover from this issue, you can clear the offending ARP entry.

Impact of workaround: This is only a temporary workaround. The issue will return if the conditions are met again.

  1. Log in to the Traffic Management Shell (tmsh) by typing the following command:

    tmsh

  2. Display the list of dynamic ARP entries by typing the following command:

    show net arp dynamic

  3. Clear the offending ARP entry by using the following command syntax:

    delete net arp <ip address>

    For example:

    delete net arp 10.10.10.10

Once the offending ARP entry has been cleared, a subsequent network-traffic-loss event on the VLAN of the active vCMP guest will trigger a failover event, as expected.
