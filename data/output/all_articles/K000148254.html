Topic

This article applies to the TLS stack used by the Traffic Management Microkernel (TMM) in BIG-IP Next platforms.

Description

This article applies to BIG-IP Next beginning in 20.3.0. For information about BIG-IP versions, refer to the following articles:

  • K000136126: SSL ciphers supported on BIG-IP platforms (16.1.x - 17.1.x)

Supported Diffie Hellman groups

DH Groups specify the Elliptic Curve groups (ECDHE), Finite Field groups (FFDHE), and Brainpool curves used in the TLS key agreement algorithm to determine the strength of the key used in the key agreement process. These are only applicable to Diffie Hellman key agreements. The algorithms are separated by a colon :, For example, P256:X25519

Supported signature Algorithms

Signature Algorithms specify the digital signature algorithms used for authentication. It is a combination of hashing algorithm (ex. SHA256) and a cryptographic hash signing algorithm. The latter could be RSA (with PSS or PKCS padding), DSA, Elliptic Curve, or SM2/3. The algorithms are separated by a colon :. For example: RSA_PKCS1_SHA256:ECDSA_P256_SHA256

Additional Information

  • A Cipher String specifies a combination of cipher suites, separated by a colon :. For example: ECDHE-RSA-AES256-GCM-SHA384:ECDHE-RSA-AES256-CBC-SHA:ECDHE-RSA-AES256-SHA384:AES256-GCM-SHA384
  • Key Exchange is the algorithm used to negotiate the session keys used for bulk encryption. The RSA algorithm alone can be used for both handshake and authentication, however does not provide forward secrecy. Modern forward secret ciphers thus use a combination of separate key exchange and authentication algorithms, typically some form of Diffie Hellman (e.g. ECDHE, ECDH, EDH, DHE) for key exchange, and either RSA or ECDSA for authentication.
