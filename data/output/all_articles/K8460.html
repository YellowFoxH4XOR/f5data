K8460: Third-party client certificates with expirations after 1/19/2038 are handled correctly

Topic

For 32-bit UNIX and Linux operating systems and products based on them, the data structure used to store the current time (in seconds past January 1, 1970) will reach its maximum value and roll over. For a 32-bit structure (signed integer: one signed bit and 31 data bits). Programs that use this data structure may fail to function correctly when they encounter dates after January 19, 2038. This is known as the "Year 2038 Problem", similar to the "Y2K Problem". 

Client certificates that are created by third-party certificate authorities with expiration dates (the valid to field of the certificate) beyond January 19th, 2038 are correctly handled by the FirePass controller.  FirePass client-side components that gather information on client certificates for session variables, pre-logon sequences and protected configurations are not affected by the "Year 2038 Problem". The FirePass controller correctly handles certificate information sent by client-side components.
