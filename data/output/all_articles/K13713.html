Known Issue

The VIPRION system incorrectly sends traffic to a non-existent or non-operational blade after 24.9 days. This issue occurs when one of the following conditions is met:

  • The VIPRION system has two or more blades previously installed and one of the blades was removed, but a replacement blade was not inserted for 24.9 days.
  • The VIPRION system has two or more blades installed and the TMMs on one of the blades has been stopped for 24.9 days.

This issue occurs because an unsigned 32-bit millisecond counter that tracks the status of the blades wraps around after 24.9 days. As a result, the remaining blades in the chassis assume that the non-existent or non-operational blade is functioning and attempt to send traffic to the non-existent blade for processing.

Impact

The VIPRION system may intermittently drop traffic.

Symptoms

As a result of this issue, you may encounter the following symptoms:

  • The VIPRION system may intermittently drop traffic
  • The status of the non-existent blade may be reported as Slot Failed instead of Slot state unknown
  • The TMMs on the functioning blades may fail to communicate with the TMMs on the non-existent blade and log error messages to the /var/log/tmm file that appear similar to the following example:

    notice MPI stream: connection to node 127.10.3.3 lost

Workaround

For a VIPRION system that has already encountered this issue, you can recover the system by rebooting.

If you have removed a blade, but have not replaced it within the 24.9 days, you can prevent this issue from occurring by rebooting the VIPRION system. To do so, enter the following command from the command line:

Impact of workaround: The VIPRION system will temporarily fail to process traffic and failover if configured as part of a high availability system.

clsh reboot
