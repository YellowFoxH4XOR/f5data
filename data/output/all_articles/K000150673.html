Misinformation

Misinformation occurs when large language models (LLMs) produce false, misleading, or biased information. This vulnerability can lead to inaccurate medical advice, reputational damage, and legal liability. One of the major causes of misinformation is hallucination when the LLM does the following:

  • Generates fabricated information but presents it as factual
  • Ignores specific instructions given in the user input
  • Includes information not present in the provided context

There are several factors contributing to hallucinations in LLM:

  • Biases introduced by training data; LLMs are typically trained with large numbers of diverse datasets from different origins which are challenging to verify their factual correctness.
  • Incomplete contextual information in the training data may contribute to hallucinations during the LLM inference stage due to defective decoding strategies and inherent randomness in the sampling methods.
  • Vaguely worded prompt or prompt without context may lead to an LLM filling gaps with invented data and generating hallucinated responses.

Misinformation attack scenario

Examples of LLMs propagating misinformation include but not limited to the following:

  • When a lawyer uses an LLM-powered chatbot to draft a legal document for submission to court, the chatbot includes fabricated cases in its output and the lawyer submits without verification. This may lead to severe repercussions for the lawyer, both professionally and legally.
  • During the COVID-19 pandemic, there were many misinformation about various treatments. If the LLM was trained on such data, then the app using the LLM would continue to propagate these incorrect information.

General strategies for mitigating misinformation attacks

To mitigate the risk of misinformation, a defense-in-depth approach is recommended. This involves multiple layers of security controls and measures:

  • Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG): Use RAG during pre-processing to retrieve relevant and verified information from trusted external databases and integrate the retrieved information into the LLM to enhance the context of the topic during response generation. This helps mitigate the risk of hallucinations and misinformation.
  • Model fine-tuning: Use techniques such as parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) and chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting to help reduce the incidence of misinformation. PEFT adds a few parameters while preserving most of the parameters for a predetermined specific task; this helps to train the LLM more efficiently (compared with traditional fine-tuning) without having to start from scratch. CoT prompting guides the LLM to break down a complex task into a series of smaller steps and incorporate logical/reasoning steps in its output.
  • Data validation and sanitization: Implement data validation and sanitization techniques to ensure that the LLM-generated response is accurate and free from biases.
  • API and app security: Use API and application security measures to scrutinize and filter the output from LLMs before it is sent to the end user, thus reducing the risk of misinformation.

Summary

Misinformation from LLMs is a significant vulnerability that can lead to security breaches, reputational damage, and legal liability. It is primarily caused by hallucinations, biases in training data, and overreliance on LLM-generated content. To mitigate this risk, F5 recommends a defense-in-depth approach involving multiple layers of security controls and measures. This includes retrieval-augmented generation, model fine-tuning, data validation and sanitization, and API and app security.
