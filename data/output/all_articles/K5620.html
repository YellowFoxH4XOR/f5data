Topic

When you perform a packet trace, either on BIG-IP or 3-DNS or on another device, you may observe packets that are sent by BIG-IP or 3-DNS that do not appear to meet the minimum Ethernet frame size of 64 bytes.

You may observe short frames under the following conditions:

  • When you perform a packet trace on BIG-IP or 3-DNS and specify a VLAN as the interface, you may see frames that do not meet the minimum Ethernet size because the frames are not padded to the required length until they pass to the physical interface. If you perform a packet trace on the interface device, rather than the VLAN, you should observe frames that meet the required length.

  • When you perform a packet trace on a device other than the BIG-IP or 3-DNS, there are several reasons why frames may not appear to meet the minimum Ethernet size:

    • The 4 byte Frame Check Sequence is generally removed from the frame when the frame is received by the network interface. As a result, the frame that is passed to the software may be as small as 60 bytes.
    • Tagged frames may be passed to the packet capture software without the tags. This can result in a frame that appears 4 bytes smaller than when it was sent.
    • Depending on the parameters used, the packet capture software may remove the padding from the frame. This can result in a packet that is several bytes smaller than the required Ethernet length.

    When combined, these conditions may allow for Ethernet frames that appear to be as small as 50 bytes, although they met the required minimum size when they were sent.
