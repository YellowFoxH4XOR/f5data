K000162752: False positive reboot alarm

Type
Support Solution
Published
2026-08-04
Updated
2026-08-04
Applies to
  • F5OS Products
Source
https://my.f5.com/manage/s/article/K000162752

Description

Zabbix raises a reboot alarm even though F5OS-A and the BIG-IP tenant remain operational. The monitored value is DISMAN-EVENT-MIB::sysUpTimeInstance, and a counter decrease or reset is interpreted as a reboot.

DISMAN-EVENT-MIB::sysUpTimeInstance = Timeticks: (16516174) 1 day, 21:52:41.74

Environment

  • F5OS-A
  • SNMP monitoring through Zabbix
  • Reboot monitoring configured against the standard SNMP sysUpTime value

Cause

The 32-bit SNMP sysUpTime counter can roll over after approximately 497.1 days of continuous operation. When the counter returns to a low value, Zabbix may detect the decrease and incorrectly interpret it as a device reboot.

F5OS-A uptime and BIG-IP tenant uptime can remain normal after the SNMP sysUpTime counter rolls over.

Recommended Actions

  1. Review the Zabbix item and trigger configuration for the reboot alarm. Confirm whether it monitors the standard SNMP sysUpTime value.
  2. Do not use a decrease or reset of sysUpTime alone as definitive evidence of an F5OS-A host reboot.
  3. For long-term uptime monitoring, use the snmpEngineTime OID:

    .1.3.6.1.6.3.10.2.1.3.0

  4. Update the Zabbix trigger logic to account for counter rollover and prevent a normal rollover from being treated as a reboot.
  5. Correlate suspected reboots with F5OS-A uptime, BMC system-event records, BIG-IP tenant status, and relevant system logs.
  6. For explicit F5OS-A system-reboot trap monitoring, use the reboot trap OID when supported:

    .1.3.6.1.4.1.12276.1.1.1.327681

  7. The reboot-specific trap is available in F5OS-A releases that provide this fix. Upgrade from releases without the trap support, then configure the trap in Zabbix.

Additional Information

  • A low or reset sysUpTime value does not necessarily indicate that F5OS-A or the BIG-IP tenant rebooted.
  • A standard Linux uptime value can continue to show long-term operation after the SNMP sysUpTime counter rolls over.
  • snmpEngineTime is a more suitable metric for long-term uptime monitoring than the 32-bit sysUpTime counter, provided that the correct OID and rollover handling are configured.
  • A reboot-specific SNMP trap is preferable when the monitoring requirement is to detect an actual F5OS-A system reboot.
