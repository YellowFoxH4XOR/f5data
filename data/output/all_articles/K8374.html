Issue

When you install a new SSL certificate on the Device Management > Security > Certificates page in the FirePass Administrative Console, FirePass may report an error message that appears similar to the following example:

Your certificate chain cannot be fully verified.

This message occurs when one of the following four conditions are met:

  • You did not paste a certificate chain in the Optionally, put your intermediate certificate chain here (in the PEM format) field.
  • You pasted an invalid or improperly formatted certificate chain in the Optionally, put your intermediate certificate chain here (in the PEM format) field.
  • You pasted a valid certificate chain in the Optionally, put your intermediate certificate chain here (in the PEM format) field, but you did not paste the certificates in the correct order.
  • The issuing root certificate authority (CA) is not part of the FirePass trusted root CA certificate file. For example, a self signed certificate.

    In this circumstance you can safely save the certificate to FirePass.

When pasting a certificate chain, you must paste the Root CA certificate and all Intermediate CA certificates provided by the CA that issued your SSL certificate. The certificates must be in PEM format, and typically you paste the certificates in the order of trust (starting with the Root CA certificate).

For example, if Network Solutions provides the CA certificates NetworkSolutions_CA.crt, UTNAddTrustServer_CA.crt, and AddTrustExternalCARoot.crt with your SSL certificate, you must paste the CA certificates in the following order:

  1. NetworkSolutions_CA.crt
  2. UTNAddTrustServer_CA.crt
  3. AddTrustExternalCARoot.crt

If your certificate was issued by GoDaddy, the intermediate certificate chain is typically located in the file gd_bundle.crt. Cut and paste the contents of gd_bundle.crt into the Optionally, put your intermediate certificate chain here (in the PEM format) field. The file gd_bundle.crt should be located in the zip file provided by GoDaddy, and it can also be obtained at https://certs.godaddy.com/anonymous/repository.seam.

In some instances, such as for Extended Validation (EV) SSL certificates recently issued by VeriSign, you should paste the certificates in the certificate chain in the opposite order, from the least trusted CA certificate (the intermediate CA certificate which issued the EV SSL certificate) to the most trusted CA certificate (Root CA certificate).

Important: If you receive the Your certificate chain cannot be fully verified error message after pasting a correctly formatted certificate chain, try pasting the certificates in the certificate chain in the opposite order.

Note: If you are unsure of which certificates to paste in the Optionally, put your intermediate certificate chain here (in the PEM format) field, or which order to use, contact the CA who issued your SSL certificate to verify the correct certificate chain.

If you are installing an Extended Validation SSL (EV SSL) certificate, you must paste the corresponding certificate chain to ensure full functionality. For more information, refer to K8330: Installing Extended Validation SSL certificates.

If you are installing a regular (non-EV SSL) SSL certificate, you have the option to include a certificate chain. If your SSL certificate was issued by a well-known Certificate Authority (CA) such as VeriSign or Entrust, a certificate chain should not be required. CA certificates from well-known Certificate Authorities are pre-installed on most modern web browsers, allowing the browsers to verify the CA digital signature on your SSL certificate. If your SSL certificate was not issued by a well-known CA, you may want to include the corresponding certificate chain to ensure that browsers can verify the CA signature on your SSL certificate.

Important: If your issuing CA provides CA certificates with your SSL certificate, F5 Networks recommends that you paste those CA certificates into the Optionally, put your intermediate certificate chain here (in the PEM format) field.

Important: If a browser cannot verify the digital signature on your SSL certificate, it will alert the end-user that your SSL certificate is from an untrusted source.

If you paste a certificate chain in the Optionally, put your intermediate certificate chain here (in the PEM format) field and receive a Your certificate chain cannot be fully verified error message, ensure that all certificates in the certificate chain are in PEM format and are valid. For example, ensure that the certificates have not expired or been revoked by the issuing CA. Additionally, ensure that no intermediate certificates are missing from the certificate chain. If you are unsure of which certificates to use, contact the CA who issued your SSL certificate to verify the correct certificate chain.
