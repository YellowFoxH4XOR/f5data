K000159681: Credential harvesting campaign targeting F5 VPN users

Type
Security Advisory
Published
2026-01-23
Updated
2026-01-23
Applies to
  • BIG-IP
  • BIG-IP APM
Source
https://my.f5.com/manage/s/article/K000159681

On January 13, 2026, researchers identified a large-scale credential harvesting campaign targeting several VPN providers, including F5. The threat actors behind the campaign registered numerous doppelgänger domains designed to mimic legitimate F5 domains. These domains are used to deceive victims into downloading counterfeit BIG-IP VPN client installers.

After the fake VPN installer is installed and executed on a victim's machine, any user credentials entered into the client are exfiltrated to attacker-controlled Command and Control (C2) servers. To maintain the appearance of legitimacy, the counterfeit VPN generates a benign error message after execution.

Furthermore, it has been observed that the threat actors leveraged trusted infrastructure such as GitHub to reinforce the legitimacy of their campaign. Malicious JavaScript steering files are hosted on GitHub, which redirected victims to F5-related doppelgänger domains. These redirections were specifically triggered when users accessed the site through certain search engines, operating systems, or browsers.

Recommended Actions

The counterfeit BIG-IP VPN client has been identified as part of a credential harvesting campaign. To mitigate the associated risks and protect your systems, we strongly recommend performing the following actions:

  1. Download BIG-IP software from only authorized sources. 
    • Ensure all BIG-IP software is downloaded solely from the official F5 website: https://my.f5.com/manage/s/downloads.
  1. Block doppelgänger domains.
    • Prevent access to the following fraudulent domains and associated IPs:
      • Domain:  *[.]big-ip-client[.]com
        IP: 172[.]67[.]182[.]218, 104[.]21[.]91[.]249
      • Domain: *[.]big-ip-client[.]net
        IP: 80[.]249[.]132[.]130
  1. Do not download BIG-IP software or scripts from unverified sources. 
    • Avoid downloading BIG-IP software or scripts hosted on:
      *[.]big-ip-client[.]github[.]io
  1. Restrict communication with the following malicious C2 servers:
    • house-connection[.]pro
      IP: 82[.]29[.]157[.]171
    • myfiles2[.]download
      IP: 172[.]67[.]175[.]165, 104[.]21[.]72[.]52
    • 5913261[.]cc
      IP: 54[.]215[.]31[.]113
    • postgresql-download[.]com
      IP: 172[.]67[.]206[.]108, 104[.]21[.]85[.]137
    • myconnection[.]pro
      IP: 82[.]29[.]157[.]171
    • vpn-connection[.]pro
      IP: 82[.]29[.]157[.]171
  1. Verify malicious script files by their hash values.

Important notes:

  • The brackets '[ ]' in the domain names were added intentionally. Make sure to remove them before adding the domains to the block list.
  • If you notice any of the above-mentioned IOCs active in your environment, immediately notify the group in your organization that manages such incidents (typically IT). Proceed according to either an existing, defined process or policy, or using the group's recommendation.

You can use BIG-IP AFM to block the malicious IP addresses or domains. For more information, refer to K10354610: Firewall rules | BIG-IP AFM operations guide.
