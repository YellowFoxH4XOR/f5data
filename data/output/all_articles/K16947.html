Topic

If you are using the high availability (HA) group feature to facilitate failover for your Sync-Failover device group, you should be aware of the recommended practice uses for the feature.

Description

An HA group is a high availability feature that allows you to specify a set of configuration objects such as trunks, pools, and VIPRION clusters that may be used to raise failover for redundant BIG-IP systems. When you associate an HA group with a traffic group instance on a specific device, the BIG-IP system calculates a health score for that device for the associated traffic group, based on trunk, pool, or cluster availability. This HA health score determines the device that should be active at any given time and then triggers failover if necessary. You should have an understanding of best practice methods when using an HA group, and be aware of BIG-IP features that may not be compatible with the HA group feature.

Recommendations

When configuring an HA group, you should consider the following factors:

  • HA group configuration is device specific and is not synced between members of a DSC group.
    • You must create a separate HA group on every device in the device group for this traffic group. 

      After you create an HA group on a device and associate the HA group with a traffic group, you must create an HA group and associate it with that same traffic group on every device in the device group.

  • When using pool objects to facilitate failover for an HA group, you should be aware of the following:
    • A pool object should not be the only element used to calculate the health score.

      F5 recommends that you use trunks and pools, or trunks only, to calculate the health score for a device.

    • The pool members should not be volatile and prone to flapping.

      The HA group feature is designed to facilitate fast failover, based on a system score. The failover triggered by an HA group is noticeably faster than a failover triggered by system, gateway, or VLAN failure. Therefore, if you rely on a pool alone to facilitate failover and the pool members are known to be volatile and occasionally flap, the BIG-IP systems may experience unnecessary failover events.

  • Do not configure the min-up-members attribute on any pool or cluster  that you intend to include in the HA group. These will likely clash with HA group operation and cause unnecessary failover events.

  • Use minimum-threshold with care. It will typically be best to leave it at the default of zero unless you intend for the device not to process traffic for a traffic-group if a specific pool, trunk or cluster member minimum-threshold isn't satisfied. This new behavior was introduced in v13.1.0 and is detailed in K68062382: HA score calculation behavior change in BIG-IP 13.0.0 and later.

  • The Auto Failback option is not compatible with the HA group feature prior to BIG-IP 13.0.0.

    For more information regarding the Auto-Failback option, refer to the Managing Failover chapter of the BIG-IP LTM BIG-IP Device Service Clustering: Administration manual.

    Note: For information about how to locate F5 product manuals, refer to K98133564: Tips for searching AskF5 and finding product documentation.

    • K15067: The Auto Failback option is not compatible with the HA group feature
    • K15495: The Traffic Management Shell may allow configuration of conflicting failover settings

  • Use of the Force to Standby feature is compatible with the  HA group feature from BIG-IP 13.1.0. Prior to that version it wasn't.
  • If the HA group score falls to  zero on the currently active unit, the active bonus will no longer be added. 
  • In rare circumstances, two or more devices may have the same HA group score. In this situation, the next-active device is chosen by a tiebreaker based on which peer has the highest failover score (also known as traffic group score). The management IP address of each device is used in calculating a score for this tiebreaker. F5 cannot guarantee that the algorithm used for these tiebreaks will not be changed at some point in the future. Thus it would be best not to rely on a particular peer always winning a tiebreak based solely on management IP addresses used when a HA group score tie is resolved. Typically it is  expected though, without further changes to how tiebreak is calculated.

    As another example if the number of monitored objects is beneath the minimum-threshold for that type, the total HA group score will be zero. If the peer HA group score is also zero perhaps due to the same or another monitored object being beneath the set minimum-threshold then the failover score will be used as a tiebreak.
