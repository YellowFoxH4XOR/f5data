Issue

The BIG-IP system taking over as the active unit during a failover event typically sends out gratuitous Address Resolution Protocols (ARPs) for each virtual address and floating address to update the ARP cache of neighboring network devices.

Old Behavior

In versions prior to BIG-IP 10.0.1, the BIG-IP system sends out six gratuitous ARPs, per address, at ten-second intervals.

Beginning in BIG-IP 10.0.1, the BIG-IP system sends out six gratuitous ARPs, per address, at one-second intervals.

New Behavior

Beginning in BIG-IP 10.2.0, by default, the BIG-IP system sends out five gratuitous ARPs, per address, at one-second intervals.

Additionally, beginning in BIG-IP 10.2.0, the arp.gratuitousrate database variable was added to provide flexibility in controlling the amount of gratuitous ARPs sent out by the BIG-IP system. For more information about the arp.gratuitousrate database variable, refer to K11985: Overview of the arp.gratuitousrate and arp.gratuitousburst database variables.

Impact

Some network devices may ignore the gratuitous ARPs sent over the shorter period. As a result, the network devices may not update their ARP cache and fail to route traffic to the appropriate BIG-IP system.

Recommended Actions

  • Verify that local network devices support gratuitous ARPs that are sent at the more frequent rate.
  • If the local network devices do not support frequent gratuitous ARPs, consider the following actions:

    • Configure the arp.gratuitousrate database variable with a value that is appropriate for your network environment. (BIG-IP 10.2.0 and later)
    • If your network environment supports MAC masquerading, you may be able to reduce the number of gratuitous ARPs sent by configuring MAC masquerading. For information about configuring MAC masquerading, refer to K7214: Configuring MAC masquerade (9.x - 10.x).
