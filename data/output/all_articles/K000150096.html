K000150096: Cannot Ping Anything on BIG-IP VE VLANs Hosted on Linux KVM

Description

You are unable to ping devices outside the vlan subnets on your BIG-IP VE hosted on a Linux KVM hypervisor. The management interface and high availability (HA) interfaces are functioning properly, VLAN traffic on your internal trunk interfaces cannot pass to external devices or switches connected to the network.

Some Symptoms are:

  • You cannot ping devices outside the local subnet on internal trunk interfaces carrying VLAN traffic.
  • Switches (e.g., Cisco) show UDLD errors on trunk interfaces.
  • Management and high availability interfaces working correctly without errors.
  • MAC addresses and physical port connections verified, but VLAN traffic routing fails.

Environment

  • Linux KVM

  • BIG-IP VE
  • Cisco Switches

Cause

Not an F5 issue. Misconfiguration of VLAN traffic handling between Linux KVM, and external switches.

  • In Linux KVM, multiple VLANs may have been grouped into a single bridge interface, with tagging expected to occur within BIG-IP VE's virtual networking layer.
  • External switches (e.g., Cisco) expected untagged VLAN traffic on trunk ports, leading to mismatched VLAN setups and UDLD errors.

Answer/Recommended Actions

Restructure VLAN and bridge configurations on the Linux KVM host to align with switch requirements.

In certain network deployments, each VLAN should have its own Linux bridge to properly isolate and segment traffic. Virtual NICs for BIG-IP VE must explicitly map to bridges carrying specific segmented traffic.  Following is an example based on a scenario where a Cisco switch was expecting untagged traffic:

On KVM host:

  1. Tie the physical trunk interface to a bridge
  2. Create the VLAN interfaces and tie them to individual bridge interfaces.

On the BIG-IP VE:

  1. Assign each Linux bridge interface to the BIG-IP VE interface.
  2. Configure trunks on the BIG-IP VE
  3. Assign VLANs to the trunks, ensuring they are set to untagged.
