K000149967: Wildcard virtual server traffic flow differences for 1-nic and multi-nic VE deployments

Description

The behaviour of wildcard virtual servers with IP traffic targeted to a self-IP is different on 1-nic VE deployments as compared to multi-nic VE deployments.

This means that a working config created on a 1-nic VE deployment may not work in the same way on a multi-nic VE deployment.

Environment

  • BIG-IP LTM VE in Cloud environments
  • 1-nic or multi-nic deployments

Cause

On traditional multi-nic deployments (BIG-IP appliances and BIG-IP VE) there is an implicit bigself listener for each self-IP that captures IP traffic with the self-IP as the destination. This bigself listener then applies the self-IP Port Lockdown settings, and the allowed packets are passed through tmm to processes on the linux host.

 

On 1-nic deployments (BIG-IP VE only), there is no bigself listener, so all traffic (apart from ssh on port 22 and webui https on port 8443) is passed to tmm to match virtual servers.

On 1-nic deployments with a 0.0.0.0:0 listener, IP traffic destined for the self-IP will be accepted by tmm and matched to the 0.0.0.0:0 listener.

 

On a multi-nic deployment, IP traffic destined for the self-IP will be accepted by tmm and matched to the bigself (<self-IP>:0) listener.

This traffic will not match the 0.0.0.0:0 listener. This is defined by the virtual server order of precedence rules defined in K14800, where the more specific listener (<selfIP>:0) take precedence over the less specific listener 0.0.0.0:0.

Recommended Actions

On multi-nic deployments you should create <selfip>:<port> listeners that are more specific than the bigself listener (<selfIP>:0).
