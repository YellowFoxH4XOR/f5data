K32545132: ARP entry in BIG-IP stays incomplete on physical BIG-IP devices

Type
Support Solution
Published
2020-02-05
Updated
2025-05-03
Applies to
  • BIG-IP
  • BIG-IP LTM: 16.X.X, 17.X.X, 17.1.X, 17.1.2, 17.1.1, 17.1.0, 17.0.X, 17.0.0, 16.1.X, 16.1.5, 16.1.4, 16.1.3, 16.1.2, 16.1.1, 16.1.0, 16.0.X, 16.0.1, 16.0.0
Source
https://my.f5.com/manage/s/article/K32545132

Description

  • An ARP entry on the BIG-IP system remains in an 'incomplete' state, even though an ARP response is received.
  • Comparing the ARP response captured on the sending device with the same packet seen by tmm on the BIG-IP shows a single bit flipped (could be anywhere in the packet, but is often seen in in the ARP MAC address)
  • The incomplete ARP entry can produce secondary symptoms with network connectivity problems reflected in the status of e. g. the Config Sync, health monitor(s), etc.
  • Wireshark and/or tshark may interpret this packet as containing an "unknown ARP opcode" if the bit flip occurs at that location in the packet, or may warn of a "duplicate use of <IP> detected" as one IP shares two different MAC addresses, the correct one and the one with the flipped bit.

Note: These bit flips are not limited to ARP packets. They have also been seen in other parts of frames (TCP header, TCP payload, etc.) resulting in packet drops due to bad checksum, TCP resets, flapping of the HA connection, flapping health monitors, etc.

Environment

  • BIG-IP
  • A BIG-IP hardware platform that containst an HSB (High Speed Bus) chipset

Cause

This issue may be caused by Bug ID 891333 and/or Bug ID 385481, which both track manifestations of single bit flips occurring within the HSB hardware.

Recommended Actions

Open a support ticket with F5 to investigate further.

Workaround
If the HSB is the cause of the issue, power cycling the affected BIG-IP system should clear the problem. However, doing so will limit F5's ability to collect necessary troubleshooting data.

Note: If this issue occurs on vCMP Guest, the psysical vCMP Host system will need to be rebooted.

Additional Information

Taking simultaneous packet captures (e. g. with "tcpdump" utility) on client and BIG-IP allows to see the bit fipped in the MAC address.
