Description

BIG-IP VE runs on hypervisor (eg. VMware ESX, KVM) in customer's environment. If the hypervisor does not provide CPU resources to the BIG-IP VE in a reliable manner, it can lead to CPU starvation on the BIG-IP VE. Since BIG-IP VE does not have direct access to the hypervisor, it can be difficult to determine if the hypervisor is the cause of unexpected symptoms. This article explains the steps to verify hypervisor issue and how to resolve it.

Possible symptoms
  • '01010029:5: Clock advanced by <number> ticks' log messages
  • unexpected failover occurred
  • devices became active-active
  • DSC (configsync) or HA mirroring connections interrupted
  • unexpected failures of multiple health monitors
The main symptom to look out for is the 'Clock advanced by <number> ticks' message. In BIG-IP VE, this message may indicate that TMM (and BIG-IP VE as a whole) was starved of CPU resources by the hypervisor. The number of ticks is expressed in miliseconnds (1000 ticks = 1 sec). If the number of ticks exceed 3000 (3 sec), it may have an adverse effect. (Default network failover timeout is 3 sec, and typical health monitor's interval is 5 sec.) The higher the ticks, the more severe is the impact.

Environment
  • BIG-IP VE
  • hypervisor (eg. VMware ESX, KVM)
Cause
  • resource intensive operation running in the hypervisor (eg. backup, snapshot)
  • the BIG-IP VE does not have high priority or CPU resources reserved in the hypervisor
  • busy or overloaded hypervisor
Recommended Actions

Verify if CPU starvation occurred and if it caused the symptoms

The messages we are concerned about are contained in /var/log/ltm. This log file can be large and verbose, and the pattern may not be easily observed without log filtering. Therefore, we provided some suggested commands below to highlight the correlation between 'Clock advanced' messages and the unexpected symptoms. You may modify the command to suit your situation, or review /var/log/ltm in its entirety to identify suspicious messages.

  4. Verify if CPU starvation caused other symptoms

    Run the following CLI command on the BIG-IP VE.

    grep -P 'Clock|<keyword 1>' /var/log/ltm*

    You may add more keywords by adding '|<keyword n>' to the end of the grep pattern, eg.

    grep -P 'Clock|<keyword 1>|<keyword 2>|<keyword 3>' /var/log/ltm*

Resolution

Once the correlation between CPU starvation ('Clock advanced' messages) and the unexpected symptom has been established, the hypervisor administrator will need to investigate further into the hypervisor to resolve the issue. There is no settings on the BIG-IP VE that can be adjusted to prevent CPU starvation as the cause is external.

Some areas the hypervisor administrator can look into:
  1. Check if there was any intensive task performed during the period. Common intensive tasks reported by customers include performing backup or taking snapshot.
  2. Increase the priority or enable CPU reservation for the BIG-IP VE in the hypervisor (the common default value for CPU reservation among hypervisors is none). For VMware, refer to https://support.f5.com/kb/en-us/products/big-ip_ltm/manuals/product/bigip-ve-setup-vmware-esxi-13-1-0/4.html .
  3. Some customers reported increasing memory allocation to the BIG-IP VE helped. This may be specific to the customer's hypervisor environment and may not be effective in every case.
  4. Consider moving the BIG-IP VE to a cluster/host that is less crowded/busy.
