Description

When Timestamp Cookies (aka TSCookies) are enabled, idle connections will fail after approximately 2 hours and 19 minutes.

Note that under certain conditions Timestamp Cookies can be automatically enabled during upgrade to v17.1.x due to Bug ID 1691505, which is fixed in v17.5.0.

Environment

  • BIG-IP AFM
    • Timestamp Cookies enabled
  • Connections idle for long periods

Cause

Timestamp cookies make TCP Timestamps advance at 256 times the typical rate of 1 per millisecond. This causes the timestamp to roll over in approximately 2 hours 19 minutes, and connections will fail when a client attempts to resume data transfer.

This issue is tracked as Bug ID 1818765.

Recommended Actions

If you require connections to be idle for long periods, you must disable Timestamp Cookies. You can do so in tmsh using the following commands:

  1. modify security dos device-config dos-device-config dos-device-vector { tcp-ack-ts { tscookie disabled } }
  2. save sys config

Additional Information

Timestamp Cookies are a proprietary F5 feature. BIG-IP shifts the timestamp value left 8 bits to store the cookie value in the low 8 bits. This causes the timestamp to be multiplied by 2^8 or 256, so that each 1ms step advances the timestamp by 256 instead of 1.

When timestamps advance at a rate of 1 per millisecond, rollover occurs after 24.9 days. However, the timestamp is invalidated after 24 days.
