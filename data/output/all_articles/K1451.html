Topic

When BIG-IP mirrors traffic, it adds a special VLAN tag to facilitate the internal routing of the traffic. If the original traffic did not have a VLAN tag, you may notice a difference in the way your packet sniffer decodes the packets.

If the sniffer has an 802.1q (Ethertype 8100) decoder, the output on the sniffer will display the 802.1q VLAN tag. If the sniffer does not have an 802.1q decoder, the packet will appear in raw hex. Following is an example of a packet in raw hex.

11:47:22.18126564 0:1:da:08:02:1a 0:80:5f:aa:bb:cc 8100 60:
1000 0800 140a 0a0a 0050 ...

In this example, the packet is Ethertype 8100 and the VLAN tag is 1000 in hex, which is VLAN 4096 on BIG-IP.

The remaining bytes are the encapsulated Ethertype of 0800, the IP address, TCP port and data of the packet (which has been omitted in this example for brevity).
