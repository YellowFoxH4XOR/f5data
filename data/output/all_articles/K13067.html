Topic

In 2012, as an industry-wide initiative, the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) recommended to begin transitioning to 2048-bit SSL keys. Furthermore, many certificate authorities (CAs) only issue 2048-bit or larger SSL keys. As a result of this initiative, you may need to re-assess equipment and capacity to accommodate the larger key size.

Impact

The transition to larger key sizes may adversely impact CPU usage on systems in which CPU utilization is at, or near, peak capacity. The transition may also impact future upgrade and growth schedules for BIG-IP equipment.

For example, the BIG-IP system can process approximately one-fifth the number of SSL transactions per second (TPS) when switching from a 1024-bit certificate to a 2048-bit certificate; if a BIG-IP system had a capacity of 5000 SSL TPS with 1024-bit keys, you can expect 1000 SSL TPS with 2048-bit keys.

Note: F5 Product Development has not yet completed performance testing for the impact of 4096-bit keys. However, they have determined the theoretical impact could be as much as a 17x performance decrease over 1024-bit keys.
