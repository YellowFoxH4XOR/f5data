What is a Vertical DDoS Attack? How to mitigate a Vertical DDoS Attack?

 

Environment

Answer/Recommended Actions

What is a Vertical DDoS Attack?

  • A vertical DDoS attack is a type of DDoS attack that targets a range of IP addresses (such as a /24 subnet) rather than a single /32 address. The attack distributes malicious traffic across multiple IPs within the targeted range. This makes detection significantly more difficult because traditional alerting systems monitor traffic per individual /32 destination hosts. So while the aggregate attack might be 1 Gbps hitting a /24, each individual IP may only see 3-5 Mbps, falling below alert thresholds and evading detection.

How to alert/mitigate Vertical DDoS Attack?

  • We offer opt-in tenant-level alerting for customers to detect vertical attacks targeting IP ranges. This enhanced monitoring aggregates total traffic across the entire tenant in addition to standard per-host (/32) monitoring. We have the ability to tune vector-specific thresholds at both the tenant level and host level, providing two sets of thresholds for all alerting vectors. The SOC configures these tenant-level thresholds based on total traffic patterns observed, enabling detection of attacks that spread across multiple IPs and would otherwise evade per-host alerts.
  • Importantly, the countermeasures for blocking vertical attacks are identical to standard DDoS mitigation—this is not a concern. As long as we can alert correctly and detect these distributed attacks, the mitigation itself is approached the same way and is easily handled by our existing systems.
  • To enable tenant-level alerting, open a ticket with the SOC and we'll begin the discussion to configure appropriate thresholds for your environment.
