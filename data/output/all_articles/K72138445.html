K72138445: An ICMP monitor may incorrectly mark down pools or nodes in a non-default route domain

Known Issue

Internet Control Message Protocol (ICMP) monitors may mark down pools and their pool members or nodes even though they are actually up.

This issue occurs when all of the following conditions are met:

  • You have a BIG-IP system configured with a non-default route domain. The default route domain is 0.
  • You have one or more of the following configured in the non-default route domain:
    • A pool member with a gatweway_icmp monitor
    • A node with a gateway_icmp monitor or ICMP monitor
  • You delete the non-default route domain from the configuration.
  • You recreate the non-default route domain.

A non-default route domain (for example, route domain 9) may have pools or nodes monitored by ICMP monitors residing in it. When you delete this route domain, the pools, its pool members, and its nodes are correctly marked down by the monitor. However, if you recreate the route domain, the ICMP monitors for that route domain continue to incorrectly mark down the pools and their pool members or nodes.

Impact

Pools and their pool members or nodes in the non-default route domain are incorrectly marked down and the resources are unavailable for load balancing.

Symptoms

As a result of this issue, you may encounter one or more of the following symptoms:

  • You notice that pools and their pool members or nodes that are monitored by ICMP monitors are marked down even though they are up.
  • You observed that ping commands to the pool members or nodes from the BIG-IP system succeed even though the ICMP monitors continue to mark down the pools and their pool members or nodes.

Workaround

To correct this issue, perform the following procedure:

Restarting the bigd process

Impact of workaround: Restarting the bigd process temporarily interrupts the BIG-IP system monitor processing, which may cause an interruption in traffic processing.

  1. Log in to the TMOS Shell (tmsh) by typing the following command:

    tmsh

  2. Type the following command to restart the bigd process:

    restart sys service bigd

To avoid this issue, before deleting the route domain, perform one of the following procedures:

  • Removing an ICMP monitor from a pool
  • Removing an ICMP monitor from a node

Removing an ICMP monitor from a pool

Impact of workaround: Removing an ICMP monitor from a pool sets the status of pool members to Unknown and makes them unavailable for load balancing.

  1. Log in to the Configuration utility.
  2. Navigate to Local Traffic > Pools > Pool List.
  3. Click the pool from which you want to remove the ICMP monitor.
  4. Under Configuration, in the Active box, click ICMP and move it to the Available box.

    Note: If you don't want to leave the pool without a monitor, you can optionally assign a different monitor. For example, tcp.

  5. Click Update.

After you remove the ICMP monitor, you can delete the route domain and, if you recreate the route domain, the ICMP monitor will not incorrectly mark down the pool and its pool members.

Removing an ICMP monitor from a node

Impact of workaround: Removing an ICMP monitor from a node sets the status of nodes to Unknown and makes them unavailable for load balancing.

  1. Navigate to Local Traffic > Node > Node List.
  2. Click the node from which you want to remove the ICMP monitor.
  3. Under Configuration, in the Health Monitors list, click None.
  4. Click Update.

After you remove the ICMP monitor, you can delete the route domain and, if you recreate the route domain, the ICMP monitor will not incorrectly mark down the node.
