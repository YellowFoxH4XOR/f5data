Description

  • I need a step-by-step summary of how to create an ROA (Route Origin Authorization) for the RIPE IRR.

Environment

  • RIPE: https://my.ripe.net

Answer/Recommended Actions

It is suggested that you register the largest prefix (ie. summarized routes) first… and only add additional ROA’s for more specific prefix’s in an “as needed” special use case.

Registering your route object:

  1. Login to the Website Portal for RIPE: https://my.ripe.net

    NOTE: If you haven't before, you’ll need to setup an account and get it verified.  This usually requires approval by an authoritative source to connect a new account to an existing company / ASN.)

  2. On the left side click Resources >> My Resources . You will get a list of the prefix’s that are associated with your account for the ASN’s you manage. You can review items here.

  3. Under Resources, click on the RPKI Dashboard

  4. Begin an ROA process by clicking next to the prefix’s you wish to be covered by the ROA:

  5.  Click Create ROA’s for selected BGP Announcements

  6. RIPE NCC can create Certificate Authority for your LIR. Read the Terms and Conditions carefully in Article 2, and if you wish that RIPE NCC manages your ROA, choose the Hosted type of certificate authority.
  7. Accept the Certificate Authority to continue.
  8. In RPKI Dashboard, click the BGP Announcements tab.
    Here, you can find all AS numbers that are currently announcing your IP resources. To issue a new ROA, click the Route Origin Authorisations (ROAs) tab and click +New ROA.

  9. Enter the following information:

    • AS number that announces your IP resources via BGP

    • Prefix that you want to be announced

    • The most specific prefix length you desire for this ROA should be entered.

  10. Click Save (floppy disk icon) to create and save your ROA.

  11. To publish the ROA, click the Review and publish changes pop-up and then click Publish.

Verification:

Please note that it can take a 1-2 days for these objects to show up outside of your account. Depending on how quickly your IRR udpates their database. Give it some time to verify your ROA. However if it still hasn’t shown up after 3 days or so I would contact your IRR for clarification.

    1. Verify Option #1
      - you can check from a command line (such as “terminal” on a mac, or the CMD process on windows).
      NOTE:  You might have to install the whois program for Windows.

       
      Mac example:

      whois -h whois.ripe.net <your prefix>

       

    2. Verify Option #2
      - Visit the website:  https://irrexplorer.nlnog.net/
      - Enter your ASN#  (should give you an overview of all your Prefix's)
      - Note the columns which tells you which IRR the object is registered with.
      - There is also a column called "RPKI" for Resource Public Key Infrastructure.  Ideally you will also be RPKI ready.

While IRR Explorer does pick up the other prefix’s being advertised, it is not necessary that every child prefix is “green” status.

You are not required to register any more than your summarized routes to be RPKI-ready, unless there are special circumstances. For example - there might be additional ASN’s allowed to originate certain child prefix’s. Then you should create another ROA for those, with these extra allowances.

Optional: RPKI Ready (Recommended!)

In addition to creating an ROA, it is recommended to also get ready for RPKI. Since you’re logging into your IRR to setup your ROA, take this opportunity to explore enabling RPKI for the same prefix’s. Although this feature is not yet being enforced by most carriers, it is a security enhancement that will likely be enabled in the future.

If you can set it up now, then you’ll be ready for when carriers DO enforce the RPKI standard in the future.
Please note – with Ripe.net, they manage the RPKI certification as a hosted service. It makes it easier than some to be RPKI ready.
