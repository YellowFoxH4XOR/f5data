Description

  • You want to conduct penetration testing against a F5 Distributed Cloud Platform environment.
  • This document augments the F5 Acceptable Usage policy: https://support.f5.com/csp/home , as well as legal regulations.

Environment

  • F5® Distributed Cloud

Policy

Customers or authorized delegates may conduct penetration testing against their own Distributed Cloud (XC) properties, as long as the tester has authorization to do so.

Customers may NOT conduct offensive security testing against any endpoint they do not own.

Example of Acceptable and Unacceptable penetration testing scenarios:

  • Allowed: Penetration testing or other offensive testing of an application deployed through an XC Load Balancer (LB) / Virtual IP (VIP) within the customer's tenant.
    • Best practice dictates that any penetration testing activity have a clearly defined scope to prevent unintended impact to the application.
  • Allowed: Vulnerability and application security scans against applications deployed through an XC LB/VIP within the customer tenant.
  • Allowed: Reconnaissance, port scanning or related activities targeting an IP range owned by the customer and advertised through XC.
  • Prohibited: Launching attacks against XC infrastructure, including the Console
  • Prohibited: Indiscriminate scanning or attacking of XC IP ranges 
  • Prohibited: Volumetric DDoS simulations without proper authorization from F5
  • Prohibited: Targeting any endpoint outside the customer tenant environment

Any offensive security testing activities must be authorized by the owner of the targeted system.  A penetration test conducted without proper authorization is simply an "attack", and is illegal in most jurisdictions.
