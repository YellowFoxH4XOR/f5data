Description

You noticed your BIG-IP VE device experienced high CPU load and then suddenly went down. You observed similar logs in /var/log/kern.log:

May  6 18:50:54 <device_name> crit kernel: EXT4-fs (dm-3): This should not happen!! Data will be lost

2025-05-07T10:06:43+02:00 <device_name> notice boot_marker : ---===[ HD1.2 - BIG-IP 17.1.2.1 Build 0.0.2 ]===--

May  7 10:06:43 <device_name> info kernel: kernel: [    0.000000] Hypervisor detected: VMware

 

Additionally, you can observe similar messages, one per day in /var/log/kern.log:

1May 6 15:49:53 <device_name> notice kernel: EXT4-fs (dm-3): error count since last fsck: 4
2May 6 15:49:53 <device_name> notice kernel: EXT4-fs (dm-3): initial error at time 1700076459: ext4_mb_generate_buddy:757
3May 6 15:49:53 <device_name> notice kernel: EXT4-fs (dm-3): last error at time 1739872921: ext4_mb_generate_buddy:757
4May 6 15:49:53 <device_name> notice kernel: EXT4-fs (dm-1): error count since last fsck: 5
5May 6 15:49:53 <device_name> notice kernel: EXT4-fs (dm-1): initial error at time 1700063561: ext4_journal_check_start:56
6May 6 15:49:53 <device_name> notice kernel: EXT4-fs (dm-1): last error at time 1700063648: ext4_journal_check_start:56

All of the example errors show that:

The filesystem on dm-3 has logged 4 critical errors since the last time fsck (filesystem check) was run.
The filesystem on dm-1 has logged 5 critical errors since the last time fsck (filesystem check) was run.

Which means the system is tracking that corruption or disk issues are accumulating — and it hasn’t been automatically corrected.

Environment

  • BIG-IP
  • Disk issues
  • VE

Cause

These errors are typically from things like:

  • Metadata inconsistencies,
  • Block allocation issues,
  • Incomplete writes due to crashes or power loss.

In above example, there was a filesystem corruption on EXT-4 . Possible causes are:

  • Unclean shutdown,
  • Underlying disk issues (e.g., storage controller error, bad virtual disk sectors, or write caching problems),
  • Thin provisioning or overcommit — e.g., LVM thin pools or hypervisor disk running out of space.

In this example the issue is on the Hypervisor side.

Recommended Actions

You should investigate the Hypervisor side as well as:

 

  1. Backup UCS.
  2. Use https://my.f5.com/manage/s/article/K25255894 to try and fix possibly corrupted volume.
  3. Fix any possible underlying Hypervisor issue.
  4. In case of failure use UCS to create new VE.
  5. Consider non Thin provisioning.
