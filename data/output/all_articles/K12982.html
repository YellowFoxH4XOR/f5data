Topic

The BIG-IP system supports elliptic curve cryptography (ECC) for SSL profiles, pertaining to the key exchange process within the SSL/TLS handshake.

ECC is a public-key cryptosystem derived from the difficulty of solving the elliptic curve discrete logarithm issue. One advantage of ECC is that the keys may be much shorter than comparable RSA keys. The BIG-IP regenerates the EC Parameters used to define an elliptic curve on an hourly basis.

ECC curves

The BIG-IP system supports following elliptic curve cryptography (ECC) curves (also referred to as DH Groups or Supported Groups) for use in the key exchange process within the SSL/TLS handshake:

ECC curveProtocolDescription
X25519TLS 1.2, 1.3Supported in BIG-IP 14.0.0 and later. Also referred to as curve25519. For more information, refer to RFC7748.
P256TLS 1.2, 1.3Also referred to as prime256v1, secp256r1 or NIST P-256, depending on the standards organization that chose the name. For more information, refer to RFC 4492.
P384TLS 1.2, 1.3Also referred to as secp384r1 or NIST P-384, depending on the standards organization that chose the name. For more information, refer to RFC8422.
