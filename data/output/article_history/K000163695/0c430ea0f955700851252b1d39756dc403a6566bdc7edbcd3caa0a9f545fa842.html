F5 Critical Releases are software updates delivered on a regular, predictable cadence that focus exclusively on security and bug fixes.

  • Name change from Hardened Releases: F5 previously referred to these software updates as "Hardened Releases." We updated the name to Critical Releases to better reflect the essential nature of these updates and the urgency with which customers should evaluate and deploy them.
  • No new functionality: Critical Releases do not contain new features or functionality. This narrow scope minimizes change across your environment, lowers deployment risk, streamlines testing cycles, and ensures operational stability.

What we’re asking you to do

  • Deploy Critical Releases as soon as possible. Treat these as critical updates, given the nature of the security issues they address.
    To download the Critical Release for your F5 product, go to Downloads on MyF5, sign in, then select your product group, product line, and product version. For more information, refer to K000090258: Download F5 products from MyF5.
    For a list of product versions included in the Critical Release, refer to K000163478: What are the releases for the October 14 2026 Critical Release.
  • Subscribe to F5 security announcements so you are notified of future releases.

Why deploying Critical Releases matters

F5 strongly recommends installing Critical Releases as soon as possible.

  • Attackers are increasingly chaining lower-severity vulnerabilities together into novel exploits. This means the severity rating of any single fix is a less reliable guide to the true importance of a release.
  • We ask you to treat every Critical Release as though it closes a critical gap, and to prioritize deployment accordingly.
  • Moving to a faster cadence has gone hand in hand with scaling how we validate every release. We have expanded our quality assurance capacity and AI-assisted test generation across platform functionality, upgrade paths, and critical workflows. This expanded validation is designed to reduce the risk of regressions while maintaining the pace of delivery.

Security disclosure policy and paused notifications

F5 has paused publishing regular, scheduled security notifications and advisories alongside our software releases.

  • Why disclosure is paused: Publicly detailing vulnerabilities provides threat actors with actionable information to reverse-engineer fixes and develop exploits before organizations have completed testing and deployment. Pausing disclosure protects our customers by depriving attackers of a release-day roadmap.
  • Exceptions to the pause: F5 continues to actively monitor the threat landscape. We will publish out-of-band security notifications if:
    • A vulnerability is determined to be actively exploited in the wild.
    • A vulnerability is publicly disclosed or reported by third parties.

Engineering Hotfix (EHF) Bundles

In conjunction with a Critical Release, F5 may provide co-released Engineering Hotfix (EHF) bundles.

  • Why you should know about them: EHF bundles provide proactive resolutions for select critical, non-security software defects on top of the base Critical Releases.
  • Action for customers:
    • If you are currently running an Engineering Hotfix: You should review the guidance for that scenario in K000163685: Overview of engineering hotfix (EHF) release on BIG-IP version 17.1.3.6 and 17.5.1.10 and if necessary plan to install it alongside your Critical Release update so you do not lose necessary hotfix corrections.
    • If you are running standard base releases: You only need to apply the base Critical Release. You do not need to install the EHF bundle unless you are experiencing one of the specific issues documented in its release notes.
