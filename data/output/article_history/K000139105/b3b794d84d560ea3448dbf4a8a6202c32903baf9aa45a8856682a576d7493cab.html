Description

 Overdog scheduling exceeded 1/2 timeout of 5 seconds (measured:xxxxx ms) where xxxxx > 10000 ms is present in /var/log/kern.log

Environment

  • BIGIP VE ( any module / combination of modules) in Private/Public Hypervisor/Cloud 

  • Restart, Silent reboot, Possible core ( Usually TMM  SIGABRT called by SOD ) 
  • CPU stall on the VE ( lack of logging in /var/log/x , gap in graphs in qkview ) 
  • The CPU graphs from qkview will usually show lower then 100% utilization , sometimes very low utilization . In cases where CPU is 100 utilized, additional research needed to determine whether indeed it is ONLY the hypervisor issue , or the VE is actually loading the CPU  

Cause

The message above indicates that the BIGIP VE was de-scheduled from CPU Cycles by the Hypervisor 

There might be numerous causes for the Hypervisor not to provide CPU cycles to the VE , among them :

  • Not enough CPU reservation provided to the VE during configuration from the Hypervisor side 
  • The configured CPU reservation is not enough ( see https://clouddocs.f5.com/cloud/public/v1/hyperv/hyperv_setup.html )
  • The provided RAM memory is not sufficient and/or overcommitted e.g. NOT reserved for the VE exclusively 
  • Issues on the Hypervisor , for example RAID/HDD issues causing the CPU to be stuck on I/O processes not releasing those to TMM , the following article describes such issues : https://my.f5.com/s/article/K15419693

Recommended Actions

Once the message is seen in the logs, it is already enough to ask the customer to check the correctness of the configuration of the VE in Hypervisor and/or the overall health of the Hypervisor 

If the message is discovered during TMM core evaluation , this solution should provide all reference to mitigate the case without escalating to ES

"err kernel: INFO: rcu_sched self-detected stall on CPU"

"err kernel: INFO: rcu_sched detected stalls on CPUs/tasks:"

Virtual SCSI call traces involving functions such as mptscsih_qcmd,mptspi_qcmd,scsi_dispatch_cmd,scsi_request_fn,io_schedule can indicate that the guest is waiting for virtual-disk I/O completion. Filesystem journal and writeback functions provide additional evidence of storage-related guest stalls.
