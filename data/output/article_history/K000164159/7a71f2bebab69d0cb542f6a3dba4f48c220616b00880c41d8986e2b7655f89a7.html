K000164159: Weak private keys in eMudhra intermediate Certificate Authority certificates

Type
Support Solution
Published
2026-11-10
Updated
2026-11-10
Applies to
  • BIG-IP
Source
https://my.f5.com/manage/s/article/K000164159

Description

Weak private keys in eMudhra intermediate Certificate Authority (CA) certificates could allow attackers to issue fraudulent TLS certificates that appear valid to systems trusting the associated CA. Systems with outdated CA trust bundles may continue to accept these certificates, highlighting the need to review and update the trust bundles used for outbound TLS certificate validation. For more information on suggestions and workarounds for TLS certificate validation, refer to The private key was 4: How eMudhra lost the internet’s trust and how to handle.

Environment

  • BIG-IP
  • TLS certificate validation
  • Default and custom CA certificate bundles

Cause

None

Answer/Recommended Actions

The actions or steps required to address the topic covered in the description.

  •  Review the DevCentral community article The private key was 4: How eMudhra lost the internet’s trust and how to handle it for background on the reported compromise and considerations for managing outbound TLS trust.
  • Review the CA bundles used by your BIG-IP deployment, including custom bundles, and determine how they are maintained. Use the following articles to understand how F5 provides CA bundle updates and how to configure automatic updates or perform manual updates.
    • K000160199: How F5 builds and updates the provided certificate authority bundles with BIG-IP TMOS firmware
    • K000132397: Automatic update of a certificate bundle (F5 ca-bundle and custom bundles)
    • K60612439: Manually updating the BIG-IP default CA certificate bundle file

Additional Information

Compromised intermediate CA private keys can allow attackers to issue fraudulent certificates that may be accepted by systems trusting the associated CA. Browser trust-store updates do not necessarily update the CA bundles used by servers, applications, or other services.
